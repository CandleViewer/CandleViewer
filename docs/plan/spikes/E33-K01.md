## 1. The questions and the fixed decision rule

The DoD counts four questions; the Context lists the first three, and the fourth is the resume budget.

1. **Seam.** Does a child's crash seam sit at intent-first (the slice row with its deterministic `order_link_id` committed before the send) or at an outbox relay?
2. **Schedule.** Does a polled `next_action_at` index or one task per run keep a TWAP's time across a suspended loop and a restart?
3. **Orphans.** Can a resting child be re-associated by its `order_link_id` alone, with no local rows?
4. **Resume.** How long is it from process start to each run's first correct action, against the stories' 5 seconds?

The decision rule was fixed in advance (Technical notes): "if the intent-first seam produces zero duplicate children across all four kill points **and** p95 resume < 2 s at 100 runs, it is chosen outright and the outbox relay is not pursued; if it duplicates at any seam, the outbox relay is chosen even at a latency cost". Its duplicate half is met: no duplicated and no lost child at any kill point (§3). Its latency half is not: from `ready` to the first correct action, p95 is 10.60 s at 100 runs on five UIDs and 18.32 s on one (§5). The rule picks the outbox only on a duplicate, so it does not pick it here, and it does not say what follows when only the latency half fails. This finding recommends intent-first and reports the latency as a resume-budget finding (§5). It attributes the miss to neither seam: the relay's resume was not measured on the same topology, so the miss is not called shared (BQ-7). The outbox column therefore reads "not pursued by the pre-agreed rule" (BQ-7). The reduced relay prototype (K1, R1 and R2), optional under BQ-7, was not run, and the PR lists that under its deviations. Confirming the choice while the latency half is unmet is an owner item (§11).

## 3. The seam: intent-first under SIGKILL (E0, E1 and the rider states)

**E0.** 5 clean runs of a two-slice TWAP each reached `completing`, with ids of the form `cvd-<group22>-<acct4>-01ac` and `-02ac` (5 of 5 match `OUR_ID` with the suffix `ac`), the group being the run's. A run ends at `completing`, not `completed`: `CHILDREN_TERMINAL` needs fills from E29-T05's consumer, which is not composed.

- `held` (BQ-5's rider: a venue order under the child's id fails the full match) has no kill-point cell. E5c (§7) covers it: the child went `held` in 10 of 10, the run paused with its reason, and nothing was bound or re-planned.
- From `submitting` committed to the create landing (K3 to K4), the window was 5.0 ms at p50, 52.9 ms at p95 and 465.4 ms at most, over 205 slices.
- Host load at batch 1 tripped B1's 1 s persist bound: 19 of 30 K3 restarts paged `oms_step_outcome_unknown`. B1 resynced and resumed again, and each further resume found the landed create by lookup and adopted it, with no extra create (0 duplicates).
- A replacement waited for its cancel: in 52 of the 52 RC and RCb trials that replaced, the replacement was created only after the cancel had resolved. **Recommendation: intent-first.** No point duplicated or lost a child, and the slice row is the intent: a slice id with no `orders` row was never sent, because a send needs B1's `validated` row first (24 §8.4 step 5). No cross-module transaction is needed, and E33-T01's "same transaction as the submission intent" reads as "the slice row is the intent" (§9.2).

## 5. Resume (E3)

**t0 and the anchor (BQ-3).** t0 is the OMS runtime's `ready`: every B1 order hydrated and every unsent `validated` order ended (20 §6.2 steps 1 to 5). The acceptance criterion's own anchor, the time from process start to the first correct action, is headlined beside it, with exec → `ready` as the breakdown. The first correct action is, per run and after its account's first B19 pass: its due child bound, its next deadline armed, or a pause with its reason (rate budget, held, in doubt). "First child" is the run's first create accepted at the venue after the restart; B1's resumed re-send counts, and it can precede `ready`.

**The stories as they read.** US-ALGO-005 (iceberg): "slicing resumes within 5 seconds". US-ALGO-006 (TWAP) names no figure: "the next slice time is recomputed from wall-clock rather than from process start". US-ALGO-007 (chase) names no figure: "chasing resumes only after the adopted state is confirmed". Only the OCO story, US-ALGO-002, names process start: "completes within 5 seconds of process start".

**Method.** Each account mixed waiting runs, due runs and one child in flight, the most the pipeline allows (below). - **Against the bar** (five UIDs, p95 < 2 s and max < 5 s from `ready`): one run passes; at ten runs the max of 3.12 s is under 5 s but the p95 of 3.12 s is not under 2 s; at 100 runs the p95 is 10.60 s. From process start, 5 s is missed in every cell on this host once the cold import is counted.

- **The rate budget is not what binds.** No local rate refusal; in any repeat, at most 28 requests per UID and 61 per IP in any 5 s, against the IP's 600 (24 §8.7), and at most 5 venue calls in flight, against MUSTNOT-08's 200. `10018` is not modelled by the fake.
- **What binds.** Per-account serialisation: the pipeline holds the account's entry turn from sizing through the create's answer, so at most one child per account is in flight and an account's children follow one round trip at a time. Hydration: about 35 to 54 ms of database round trips per run at 100 runs. The account's first pass: 0.66–1.62 s from `ready`, because it waits for B1's resumed requests (24 §8.5, "The first pass after a start").

**Budget arithmetic (BQ-3's rider; AS-D03).**

- At `CV_ORDER_RATE_PER_UID` = 8 creates per second, the ruling's steady rate, 100 due children on one UID need at least 12.5 s, and 20 on each of five UIDs at least 2.5 s.
- The setting has no reader. `settings.py` declares `order_rate_per_uid: int = 8`, but the adapter builds its governor at the defaults, 10 tokens and 5 per second per UID (`exchange/bybit/service.py`, `exchange/bybit/rate_limit.py`), and 24 §8.7 lists `order.create` at 10 per second. At the code's rate, E3's mix (51 creates on one UID; 57 over five, so at least 12 on one) needs at least 8.2 s and 0.4 s; at 8 per second, steady, 6.4 s and 1.5 s. The measured tails exceed those minimums, so at these sizes the binding terms are the three above; at 100 due children on one UID the rate would bind.
- Per-UID and per-IP counts, the largest in any 5 s window, are in the table's requests column (AS-D03).

## 6. Scheduler (E4)

### 6.1 The variants (BQ-1 and its rider)

There is one external scheduler, the one the owner text names ("hard deadlines and algo timing stay on the external `MonotonicScheduler`", C-2.22 item 8), and the variants reuse shipped idioms instead of inventing new ones. All share one due time per run (`next_action_at`, never all of a run's deadlines), send `SLICE_DUE` only through `statechart.gateway`, follow one catch-up rule (§6.4), and call `ClockGuard.check_host_step()` before every exchange-time read, with no new step detector.

- **V1** polls a due index every 100, 250 or 1000 ms: one owned task on a fixed phase with B19's cadence arithmetic, range-scanning a sorted `(next_action_at, run)` list that stands in for `ix_algo_runs_due`, and sending without waiting. Its lateness spreads over 0 to the period by construction.
- **V2** is the shipped B19 idiom (`oms/reconcile_job.py`: `_cadence`, lines 371 to 388, with `_spawn`, `_ended` and `stop`): one task per run, spawned through `observability.spawn`, tracked with a done-callback, cancelled and gathered at stop; each task sleeps to its run's absolute due time and sends with `wait=True` inside `asyncio.timeout`. Unlike B19, a missed tick is re-planned, not skipped.
- **V3** is the `ws/pacing.py` heap (`Pacer`): one task and a min-heap with one live entry per run, stale entries skipped against the run's current due time, the heap rebuilt past 2n + 64 entries so it stays bounded (C-2.18), the waiter woken early when an earlier due is armed, and every due entry popped and sent without waiting.
- **Clock modes** for V2 and V3: mode A (BQ-1 as written, B19's convention) anchors due times as `loop.time()` values to one anchor, re-taken only when `check_host_step()` reports a step; mode B computes every sleep from exchange time read at that wake. V1 always works like mode B. Unlabelled V2 and V3 rows are mode A. Mode B ran in the clock-step shapes and once in the loaded shape (n = 1: interval p95 483.2 ms, lateness p95 253.4 ms, GC-free interval p95 25.0 ms).

### 6.7 Recommendation, and B19's conventions

**V3 in mode B, for clock robustness.** One `MonotonicScheduler` with one entry per run, keyed by `next_action_at`, which is `ix_algo_runs_due`'s key, so the heap is rebuilt from one read at boot: Postgres is the truth and the heap a cache. Unlabelled V2 and V3 rows are mode A. Mode B ran in the clock-step shapes and once in the loaded shape (n = 1: interval p95 483.2 ms, lateness p95 253.4 ms, GC-free interval p95 25.0 ms). Mode B's case rests on the clock-step shapes, where mode A cascades: after a 15 s suspend, V3 in mode A fired 0 slices and re-planned 70 times, while mode B fired 203–207 with one re-plan per run (§6.5). Its loaded timing on this host is that single row, above mode A's repeat range on interval error and over 250 ms on lateness, and E33-Q03's baselines should repeat it. V3's timing claims are mode A: equal-best with V2 in every shape, and the lowest p95 of the five in its real-time run and in the manual-order tail. V3 also dispatches without waiting on a machine, and it is the single scheduler C-2.22 item 8 names. The heap is bounded by the admitted-runs cap (SR-E33-09), a run has at most one due event outstanding, and a gateway refusal (`overflow_policy="refuse"`) is re-armed with backoff and counted, never dropped (BQ-1).

**Whether E33-T01 reuses B19's conventions** (BQ-1's rider), so that it does not become a second host-timer implementation:

- **Task conventions: reuse.** Owned tasks through `observability.spawn`, a tracked set with a done-callback, cancel and gather at stop, gateway sends inside `asyncio.timeout`, an injected sleep and clock.
- **Clock convention: do not reuse.** B19's fixed monotonic anchor, re-taken only on `check_host_step()`, stops a TWAP dead after both kinds of host suspend (§6.5).
- **Missed-tick policy: do not reuse.** B19 skips a missed tick; INV-B6-b requires a re-plan.
- **One task per run: do not reuse.** It gives no way to move a sleeping run's due time earlier, costs a task per run, and its awaited sends cut dispatch.

Binding happens only by the whole id plus the full match against the slice row E33-T01 persists before the send (SR-E29-03, SR-E33-14), and adoption into a run was never attempted.

- A run is found by its slice row's whole id, unique over all rows, never by decoding `group22`: an `AlgoSpec` is per account with an optional leg, and a trade group may carry more than one run (BQ-10). Children take the parent's group segment with an incrementing `seq2` and the `ac` suffix (24 §10.1 rule (4)), and `seq2` comes from the allocator (24 §8.3 rules 4 and 5).
- A venue-only orphan stays an unmanaged `untracked` row (24 §19.61: no human adopt action in v1; SR-E29-12): algos never act on `untracked` orders. Adoption into a run waits for D7 (open, #2701). CX-13's "E29's D6 is open" is stale, since §19.61 resolved D6 for v1.
- **What E33-T01 must keep:** the slice row, with its whole id and terms, committed before the send. An `untracked` row under a slice's id pauses the run; it is never bound, and never a reason to mint a new id, because a lone adopted row cannot tell an order of another origin from the run's own child whose rows were lost. "A unique run-to-`group22` mapping" is not a requirement (BQ-10).
