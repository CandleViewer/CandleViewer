# E28-K01 — Per-trade override semantics and profile-change propagation: findings

- Ticket: E28-K01 (#968) · Feeds: E28-T02 (#906), E28-S05 (#1144), E28-S01 (#971), #2635 (F-01), E34-S02 (#1110), E30
- Status: **finding**; the decision is ADR-0040 (`27-adrs/ADR-0040-per-trade-overrides-and-profile-propagation.md`),
  status `proposed`; the decision table's owner and Architect sign-off is tracked on #2701.
- Prototype: `spikes/e28-override-propagation/` (`measure.py`, raw `results.json`, `README.md`). **This spike produces
  no production-shippable code; the prototype is throwaway**, never promoted, and nothing of it lands in `services/api`.
- Evidence base: `main` at `28ae3f1db`. No secret, token or account credential appears here or in the prototype.

## 1. The questions

1. **Overrides.** Which `AccountProfile` field may a per-trade override touch, and how is "narrower" judged? (US-PROF-008;
   threat model F-01, F-02; SR-E28-03, SR-E28-04.)
2. **Lifetime.** Per ticket or per session?
3. **Propagation.** How does a profile change reach an open ticket within SCR-130's 2 s, inside the frozen
   `23-ws-protocol.md` topic list, on top of #906's `ProfilesChanged` bus event?
4. **Mid-ticket edits.** What does an open ticket do when its profile changes underneath it?

## 2. Answers (summary; ADR-0040 owns them)

| Question      | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 Overrides   | All 42 `AccountProfile` fields classified: 15 `never`, 27 `narrow_only`, 0 unclassified. The bound profile's caps and risk budget are the ceiling; one resulting-leg comparator decides (size and leverage up to the caps, risk within the budget, the stop no further from entry); a value past it is refused `403 risk_limit_breached`, never clamped |
| 2 Lifetime    | Per ticket: cleared on submit, cancel and retarget; never stored on the server                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| 3 Propagation | A `system` `account_notice` with `reason: "profiles.changed"` (no topic added, no frame shape changed) triggers a REST re-fetch of `GET /exchange-accounts/{accountId}/profiles`; backstops on ticket open, `auth_ok` and a stale-submit refusal                                                                                                        |
| 4 Mid-ticket  | An explicit "profile changed — review" state; the submit-time version check is the guard, the notice only propagation; Gherkin in ADR-0040 Decision 4                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

The decision table itself is ADR-0040 Decision 1 (not repeated here, C-16.5). Its count is checked against the live
model by the prototype: `decision_table_check` = 42 profile fields, 42 classified, none unclassified, none unknown.

## 3. Evidence

### 3.1 What exists on `main`

- `GET /exchange-accounts/{accountId}/profiles` is served (`api/profiles.py:188-206`): `accounts:read`, the caller's
  `account_scope` applied in SQL, an out-of-scope account answering `404` like a missing one. `PUT` and
  `ProfilesChanged` are #906's and not on `main`.
- `system` frames are queued at once by `push_system` (`ws/gateway.py:839-853`), offered to every socket by
  `GatewayHub.broadcast_system` (`ws/gateway.py:493-498`) and cut per socket by `system_view`
  (`ws/system_filter.py:88-117`): an `account_notice` reaches a Manager or Viewer only for the accounts its
  `account_scope` lists, and nothing when none remains.
- `account_notice`'s `reason` set and `system.kind` are open (`23-ws-protocol.md` §2.3, §15.9), so a new reason code
  is additive and the frozen golden does not change. §15.9's latch rule ("the latest `reason` per account id") needs one
  paragraph saying `profiles.changed` is an event, not a state: a contract-first follow-up (ADR-0040 Decision 3).
- `TradeGroupPreview.legs[]` returns `account_profile_id` but no version, and `CreateTradeGroupRequest` names no
  expected version, and `profile_changed` is not a registered problem code: the gap ADR-0040 Decision 4 records.

**Method note.** A first run of the final prototype bound its listening socket without naming `IPPROTO_TCP`, so asyncio
did not set `TCP_NODELAY` on the accepted sockets and every HTTP round trip took about 40 ms more (Nagle against a
delayed acknowledgement). The socket now names the protocol, as uvicorn's own listener does, and the numbers above are
from the corrected run; the discarded run is not in `results.json`.

**Preview.** SCR-061's budget is 300 ms. The stand-in preview (a `Decimal` copy of 24 §9.3 behind a loopback HTTP
route) is far inside it, but it reads no Postgres, no wallet and no instrument cache, so it measures the transport and
the arithmetic only; E28-S05 and E34 measure the real `POST /trade-groups/preview`.

**Scope.** In every transport run, a change on an account outside the Manager's grants produced no frame on its socket
(`frames_for_foreign_account_change: 0`) and its `GET` answered `404`.

### 3.3 What the numbers say

- Every transport measured meets 2 s by a wide margin on this topology, including the documented 1 000 ms worst case
  for `system`. Time is not what separates them; meaning, permission and the frozen schemas are (ADR-0040 Decision 3).
- The REST re-fetch is a few milliseconds here because the store is in memory; a Postgres-backed read adds its query
  time (two reads: the account, then its profiles). The 2 s budget leaves room for that and for the Tailscale hop.

## 4. Deviation: topology

The ticket asked for the measurement "on the R3 staging topology over Tailscale". That topology is not available, so
the orchestrator directed the local dev stack (note 3 on #968). Used: one developer laptop, WSL2 (Linux 6.6, x86_64,
28 logical CPUs), Python 3.12.12; one process with two threads, each with its own event loop — a server (uvicorn 0.54.0
HTTP and WebSocket on `127.0.0.1`, an ephemeral port, the real bus and the bridge) and a client (`httpx` 0.28.1,
`websockets` 17.1) — over loopback TCP. No Postgres (an in-memory store scoped as SQL scopes it), no Tailscale, no
exchange. What is real and what is a stand-in is listed in the prototype's README. The numbers bound the in-process
and loopback cost; a staging re-run belongs to E28-S01's end-to-end test (#971), which `cv_profile_propagation_seconds`
then watches in production.

## 5. Open questions (ADR-0040 §Open questions)

OPEN-1 is resolved by the coordinator's ruling of 2026-10-25 (size may rise up to the bound profile's caps and is
refused above them). Open: OPEN-2 (leverage up to the cap, guarded by the stop-inside-liquidation check), OPEN-3
(which versions a leg records), OPEN-4 (which ticket owns the bridge). Each carries a recommendation; none is guessed.

The coordinator's three rulings of 2026-10-25 are folded into ADR-0040 Decision 1: a widening request value is refused
(`403 risk_limit_breached`), never clamped; "raise size" is allowed up to the caps and a one-click minimum above the
risk budget is refused, never rounded up (#904 QA plan (V04)); an empty allow-list without `all_symbols` allows
nothing (D11, fail closed).
