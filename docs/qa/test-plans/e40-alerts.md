# E40 Alerts and notifications: black-box test plan (slice A1)

Ticket: E40-Q01 (#1202), Part A. Status: v0.1, slice A1 of two, a draft for review by the QA lead, the E40 epic owner and one
backend engineer (the ticket's DoD). A1 holds the conventions, the case-id scheme, the trigger-mode, gating, critical-override and
channel matrices, the fixtures appendix and the traceability table. Slice A2 adds the condition-family, history and export and
RBAC cases, the five exploratory charters and the fault-injection helper spec. This plan contains no code; E40-Q01 Part B
automates it. Built on the BQ defaults; rulings pending (BQ list: issue comment 6619533872 on #1202).

## 0. Conventions

- **Expectations come from the specification, not the implementation.** Each cell cites its source:
  - `US-ALRT-nnn/k` is scenario k of that story in `docs/plan/11-user-stories.md` §24, in file order;
  - `T` is the ticket text of #1202 (its Scope and Acceptance criteria) and `E40.json` is `docs/plan/backlog/E40.json`;
  - `D01` is `docs/design/research/e40-alert-attention.md` (§5 the CO list, §6 the storm rule SR-1..SR-9);
  - `D02` is `docs/design/E40/E40-D02.md`, and `CC-n` is its §17.1 contract change request `n`;
  - `S02` is the contract record `docs/plan/24-internal-schemas.md` §19.106 **on the branch of PR #5676**
    (`git show feat/e40-s02-contract:docs/plan/24-internal-schemas.md`), not on `main` yet; `OVR` is
    `packages/protocol/notification-overrides.json` on the same branch;
  - `22` is `docs/plan/22-api-openapi.yaml` and `23` is `docs/plan/23-ws-protocol.md` (§6.2.1, §15.7), cited by operation id or section;
  - `code` is the as-built module cited `file:line` on `main` at `e2c6bfff71`; it explains how a cell behaves today and never
    overrides a specification (section 1).
- **Where sources differ, nothing is guessed.** The cell says which source it follows, the difference becomes a row of
  section 8 (Known gaps) and the cell is marked **blocked on** its owning ticket. This is the ticket's "escalated, not invented" AC.
- **Case ids:** `E40-TC-<group><nn>`. Groups: **A** trigger modes (section 2), **B** gating (3), **C** critical override (4),
  **D** channels (5), **E** condition families, **F** history and export, **G** RBAC and scoping (E, F, G are written in slice A2;
  their ids are reserved in section 7), **X** cross-cutting (section 6). Matrix cells that have no row of their own carry
  the id of their row and column: `E40-TC-A-<mode>-<condition>`, `E40-TC-C-<CO>-<mute>`.
- **[F]** after a case id marks a failure path (the ticket's AC: every Must story has one).
- **Case columns:** Case, Preconditions and fixture, Steps, Expected, Verifies (`US-ALRT-nnn/k` and the child ticket),
  Layer, Automation. **Layer** is `unit`, `contract`, `integration`, `e2e` or `a11y` (`docs/plan/03-testing-strategy.md` §2).
  **Automation** is "Q01 suite" (this ticket's Part B), a named existing test file, or `manual-only`.
- **Build status tag** (the E12 convention, `qa/plans/e12-bars-and-series-test-plan.md` §0): `shipped` is merged on `main`;
  `in-review` is an open PR; `pending` is not merged. A `pending` case is written now and never counts as passing.
  Tags here are checked on `origin/main`, 2026-11-04. **PROPOSED** marks an expectation whose source is a design not yet
  ratified by the owner (the CO list, the storm constants). A **blocked** cell names the owner ticket and is not executed.
- **Environment:** the replay feed and a fake clock, never wall-clock sleeps and never a live exchange (C-13.5, `03` §17). Staging
  runs use the demo environment only. No account id, key or balance appears in a step, fixture or evidence file.
- **Time:** every cell states its event times relative to `t0`; the fake clock supplies both the evaluator's wall clock
  (cooldown, storm, snooze, expiry, `code` `evaluator.py:195`) and the exchange event time (bar keys, `gating.py:150`). A case
  never relies on more than 5 s of real elapsed time.
- **Oracles, in order:** the stored delivery and alert rows and the audit rows > the API and WS payloads > the UI. A UI-only
  oracle is used on presentation cases and the case says so. Every firing case also names a secondary oracle, a metric or an audit
  action from the E40 tickets' Observability sections: `cv_alert_fires_total{trigger_mode}`, `cv_alert_suppressed_total{reason}`,
  `cv_alert_autodisarmed_total`, `cv_alert_delivery_total{channel,status}` (`evaluator.py:399`, `:371`, `:506`;
  `dispatcher.py:414`); audit `alert.fired`, `alert.disarmed`, `alert.snoozed`, `alert.deliveries_acked`. A pass whose secondary
  record is missing fails the case (T, Observability).
- **Budgets under test** (T, Performance notes; functional testers notice a breach, the perf scripts are E40-Q02): at most 1 s to
  record and 2 s to toast, list updates at most 1 Hz, WS cadence 250 ms (`ws_topic.py`, `test_alerts_ws_pacing.py`).
- **Defects** follow `03` §11.3. A flaky case is quarantined with a filed Bug and a named owner, never deleted or retried into
  silence (T, AC "A flaky test is quarantined").

## 2. Trigger-mode matrix (group A)

**Modes** (`AlertInput.trigger_mode`, `api/alerts.py`; default `once`): `once`, `every_time`, `once_per_bar`.
**Condition behaviours** (all four are tapes of section 9; a "tick" is one evaluation of the subscription):

| Code | Behaviour                  | Tape (fake clock, 1 s ticks, `cooldown_seconds` 60, `timeframe` 1m for `once_per_bar`)                                             |
| ---- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| T1   | true once                  | false, then true for exactly one tick at `t0`, then false                                                                          |
| T2   | true continuously, one bar | true at every tick from `t0` to `t0+10 s`, with `t0` at a bar open (all ticks in one bar)                                          |
| T3   | true across a bar boundary | true at every tick from `t0` to `t0+150 s`; `t0` = bar open + 45 s, so the tape crosses the boundaries at +15 s, +75 s and +135 s  |
| T4   | true on a revised bar      | bar B is evaluated true and fires; a later tick of the same bar B (a revision, same bar-open) is true again; then bar B+1 is false |

A level condition ("last price above 65 000") is used for T2 to T4. A `crosses_above` / `crosses_below` comparison is edge
detected per instance and per node, and never fires on the first evaluation (`rules/evaluator/nodes.py:197-210`). For the
crossing cases the T2 and T3 tapes are therefore preceded by one pre-tick below the level (one tick before `t0`), so the first
true tick is a genuine cross and gives one firing on T2 and T3 whatever the mode; case `E40-TC-A13` pins that difference. The
level-condition matrix below is unchanged.

**Expected firing count per cell** (a bare `:nnn` in this section is `gating.py`). "n (reason)" counts delivered firings; every held tick is counted on
`cv_alert_suppressed_total{reason}` and is **not** recorded as a delivery in the as-built code (section 8, G-1).

| Mode           | T1  | T2                                                   | T3                                                              | T4                                                      |
| -------------- | --- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `once`         | 1   | 1; disarmed after the first (`evaluator.py:408-410`) | 1; same                                                         | 1 (the revision finds the alert retired)                |
| `every_time`   | 1   | 1 (the other 10 ticks are cooldown, `:153-159`)      | 3: at `t0`, `t0+60 s`, `t0+120 s`; the boundaries do not matter | 1 if the revision is inside the cooldown, 2 if after it |

four bars each see a true tick (case A11). The count is written here from the gate arithmetic, and the

## 3. Gating matrix (group B)

**Gate order** is normative: `enabled`, `deleted`, `expired`, `snoozed`, `muted`, `trigger_mode`, `cooldown`, `storm`; the first closed
gate stops the candidate and later gates are not consulted (T, "Gating pipeline"; `gating.py:20-22`, `:137-162`; test
`…order_first_closed_gate_wins_and_later_gates_unchecked`). The `checked` trail on the delivery's `context.gates` lets a case
prove order, not only outcome (`evaluator.py:361-365`); the owner reads it only through the DB and never over the API (CC-2).

| Gate           | Closed when                                            | Result today (`code`)                                                                                                          | Specified result                                                                                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `enabled`      | alert disabled                                         | stop, no record, no metric label (`gating.py:139`); the evaluator has already dropped disabled alerts (`evaluator.py:238-239`) | no firing, no delivery (T, gating matrix)                                                                       |

## 9. Fixtures appendix

Each fixture is a recorded window, not a synthetic stream, so the replay exercises the real ingestion path (T, Technical notes), and
no fixture contains an account id, key or balance. `main` holds the 2026-10-05 Bybit capture
(`packages/fixtures/bybit/2026-10-05/`, its `manifest.json` and `schema_expectations.json`) and no alert-specific window; its
manifest carries **no sha256** today, so the checksum entry the ticket requires is a deliverable of the integration-suite slice
(BQ-06), registered in `packages/fixtures` manifest style so that a silently changed recording fails loudly (`fixture-drift.yml`).

**Determinism rule.** Each window is replayed three times and the sequence of `(alert id, event time, delivery id order)` is
identical; the replay clock is the fake clock, so a 60 s storm costs no wall time. The fault-injection helpers of the ticket
(force delivery failed, desktop permission denied, vocabulary unavailable, a `412` concurrent edit, a storm) are specified in A2;
they ship as a test-only module behind an environment flag that the production configuration validator refuses, with a unit test
of the guard (T, Technical notes).

**The independent oracle.** The expected counts above are re-derived from the tape by a calculation that does not call the
evaluator (the E12 approach, `qa/plans/e12-bars-and-series-test-plan.md` §0), and the case is not signed off until its derived
count equals the written one. Until the tape is chosen, a numeric expectation uses the hand-built tape in its case.
