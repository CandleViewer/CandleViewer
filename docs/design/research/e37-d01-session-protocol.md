- **Companion to:** `e37-node-graph-vs-list.md` in this folder, "the memo", whose ids (SC, F, R, P, C, G, Q, A) and sections ("memo §n") are cited; "§n" is this file; `NN` is `docs/plan/NN-*`; AC 1–AC 4 are the ticket's four acceptance scenarios (issue #828).
- **Status:** not yet run. **No participant, session, timing, score or quote exists**; the durations below are allocations within a 45-minute session (ticket Scope). This file owns the session order, the correct answers written before the first session, the codes and the choice rules; the memo cites them, and a change after the first session voids the scores.
- **Roles.** A neutral moderator who did not draft the copy under test (ticket Technical notes); when the owner is the participant the session runs **self-moderated** with the script read aloud by the note-taker, tagged as such; a note-taker logs codes and requests; the owner approves the stimuli before the first session (§6 item 1).

## 1. Purpose, probes and what is locked

The sessions test how the owner (P1) and the account managers (P2) read, reason about and would author a rule on a node canvas, against the same rules they read as a condition list — before E37-D02 draws SCR-082. The memo's five research questions are the five probes:

- **Probe 1 — reading or authoring (RQ1):** which real rules they want; how they express "A and (B or C)" today; whether they reach for the canvas or the list to _build_ and to _check_.
- **Probe 2 — default entry (RQ2):** where they expect a new rule to start and where an existing one opens.
- **Probe 3 — the concept test (RQ3 via the stimulus; ticket scope bullet 2):** read the pictured rule aloud, in two framings.
- **Probe 4 — comprehension of the four round-trip states (RQ3; AC 2):** what each state means for the rule.
- **Probe 5 — the vocabulary decision test (RQ4):** which words they use unprompted for the unit, the connection point and the connection.
- **Probe 6 — auto-layout (RQ5):** what they expect auto-layout to do to their hand-placed boxes, and how they would take it back.

**Locked — not under test:** owner decision #11 (both editors, one IR); ADR-0007 rule 4 (one root condition, ordered actions; no dataflow); the shipped form subset and the graph-only list (`rules/ir/form.py`); E36-D01's grammar and the form's four indicator words (E36-D02); E35-D01's clause labels ("Check when", "Only if (all must be true)", "Then, in order", "Acts on"); the words node · port · edge below the copy layer. A participant who disputes a locked item is recorded (request code RQ-L) and not argued with.

## 2. Participants, consent and data handling

**Participants.** The closed user set of `10` §2–§3: the owner (P1) and the account managers (P2), ≥3 sessions in total (ticket Scope), one person per session; **at least one participant who states they operate keyboard-first** (ticket AC 3 — a hard requirement, not a preference; `10`:94 wrist strain, `10`:131 keyboard-first). Record per person before the session: role, whether they have armed a rule, their current way of expressing a compound condition (a template, a note, a spreadsheet, a broker's conditional order), assistive setup (screen reader, magnifier or zoom level, keyboard-only), colour vision. The keyboard-first person's session runs with the pointer unplugged for probes 3, 5 and 6; their expectations are logged **verbatim** on the sheet (AC 3) and carried to E37-D04 as quotes, not paraphrases.

**Consent and data** (ticket Security notes; memo G8 — `04` has no research-data policy, so E36-D01 §8.1's practice applies): written consent per session covering recording, transcription and anonymised quotation; recordings and transcripts live in the owner's private storage, never in the repository, with retention stated on the consent form; quotes in the deck are anonymised to "P1" / "P2a" / "P2b"; stimuli use the demo environment, the catalogue's fictional accounts "Main" and "sub_001" and BTCUSDT; no live account identifier or key material appears in any stimulus. Sessions are conducted accessibly (ticket Accessibility notes): captions available, every stimulus also exists as text, no colour-only cue.

## 3. Stimuli and how each is prepared

Build each from the memo's defaults as approved (§6 item 1), as Penpot boards or paper cards **and** as a plain-text or read-aloud version; note the version on the session sheet.

1. **The corpus cards (Probe 1).** E36-D01's rows 1, 2, 7, 9, 11, 14 and 15 (memo §3) as the trader says them, one per card; and one blank card.
2. **Stimulus A0 (Probe 3).** The catalogue's own wireframe (`14`:1836–1847) redrawn as boxes with its labels verbatim — kept as the first reading stimulus although its rule is not a valid rule (memo F1): its answer key is memo SC-13's open conflict, so it is scored only on whether the participant can name the condition and say what `( max )` combines (memo §5.1's rule); a fail is the expected, informative result. Dropping it is the owner's call (§6 item 1).
3. **Stimulus A1 (Probe 3).** E36 row 2 drawn as its three-node graph: `Check when: a 1m bar closes` → `position is open` → `tighten the stop to 2.0 × ATR(14) from the close · only tighten`, palette on the left, the title bar `Rule: "ATR trail 2x"  [Form | Graph]  ARMED-DEMO`.
4. **Stimulus A2 (Probe 3).** The one graph-only rule the plan motivates: a shared condition `position is open` used by two gates, each feeding its own action (`11`:3733; `14`:1859's panel example), badged "graph-only" at the shared node.
5. **Stimulus B (Probe 3).** A1 and A2 again as the **outline-plus-canvas split**: the node list (`14`:1864's text connections: "ATR 14, output connects to Multiply input A" pattern) as a left outline beside the canvas.
6. **The four state cards (Probe 4).** Each a chip + tooltip, in isolation, exactly as memo §5.2 prints them: `Round-trips ✓`; `Unsupported in form view` (the catalogue's string, no tooltip, the panel title "What won't round-trip"); `Round-trip mismatch`; `Not verified`. Plus the rewrite cards (2a) "Graph-only — read-only in the form", (2b) "Graph-only construct — the form shows it read-only", (2d) "Graph-only construct — read-only in the form", each with the (2c) tooltip sentence.
7. **The word cards (Probe 5).** Three cards: node / port / edge · block / socket / wire · step / input / link; shown only after the participant has described A1 and the connect flow in their own words.
8. **The auto-layout pair (Probe 6).** A1 with three nodes deliberately mis-placed by hand, and the same graph after auto-layout; a card with `[auto-layout]` and the announcement line "Layout applied — Undo auto-layout".

## 4. Session script (45 minutes)

Budget: opening 4 · Probe 1 + 2 (corpus walk) 8 · Probe 3 (concept) 10 · Probe 6 (auto-layout) 5 · Probe 4 (states) 8 · Probe 5 (words) 6 · debrief 4 = **45 min**. Probes 3, 4 and 5 are never cut (AC 1 and AC 2 need every score); if late, cut Probe 6 to the single question, then the corpus walk to four cards. **Moderator rules:** explain nothing about round-tripping, the form subset, the vocabulary or the words before the debrief (questions go on a parking card); neutral prompts only ("say more", "what would happen next?"); never correct an answer in session; think-aloud throughout; no dragging is required of a keyboard-first participant (`05` §3.1 rule 6).

1. **Opening (4 min).** Consent (§2). Warm-up: "Tell me the last rule you wished the software had run for you. How did you write it down, if at all?"
2. **Corpus walk — Probes 1 and 2 (8 min).** Lay out the corpus cards. "Pick two you would actually run." For each: "Say it as you would to another trader." Then the blank card: "Write one with an _either … or_ in it." Ask: "If you were to build this, would you start in the form (show SCR-081's sketch) or on the canvas (show A1)? Why?" and "Once it exists, where would you open it to check it?" Code Probe 1 with K1–K3 and Probe 2 with D1–D3 (§5).
3. **Concept test — Probe 3 (10 min).** Show A0 (the catalogue's sketch): "Read this rule aloud as you would say it to another trader." Then A1 (palette-left canvas), then A2. Then B for A1 and A2: "Read it again from this view." Rotate the order A-then-B / B-then-A between sessions. Code C1–C5; log every noun the participant uses for a box, a dot and a line (feeds Probe 5).
4. **Auto-layout — Probe 6 (5 min).** Show the mis-placed A1: "You placed these boxes. You press auto-layout. What happens? What do you expect to see? If you don't like it, what do you do?" Then show the after-card with the announcement line. Code L1–L4.
5. **Comprehension — Probe 4 (8 min; AC 2).** Show the four state cards one at a time, rotating the order between sessions: "What does this mean for your rule? Can you save? Can you arm? What happens if you switch to the other editor?" Code each with its K / FB codes (§5). **Re-test within the session:** if the `Unsupported in form view` card fails criterion (i), show card (2a); if (2a) fails, show (2b); if (2b) fails, show (2d); record which card passed.
6. **Decision test — Probe 5 (6 min).** First the unprompted log from step 3. Then the three word cards: "Which set would you want to read in the product? Which word would you _never_ want here, and why?" Probe the competing meanings: "Does 'edge' mean anything else to you in this product? 'Block'? 'Step'? 'Link'?" Code W1–W4.
7. **Debrief (4 min).** The parking card; "Anything the canvas should never do to your rule?"

## 5. Measures, scoring rules and read-out

**Correct answers, written before the first session (ticket Test plan).** A **pass** has the listed codes in the participant's own words and no false belief (FB) of the probe; a **partial** (one code, no FB) is not a pass.

- **Probe 1 (RQ1).** K1 the participant builds in the form for a rule inside the subset; K2 names a construct the form refuses (several _or_ groups, "not", "at least two of", a sum or a multiple, "within the last n minutes", one condition feeding two things) as the reason to use the canvas; K3 opens the canvas to _check_ a rule. FB1 believes the form cannot express a single "either … or" (it can — `form.py`; `11`:3697). H1 is **confirmed** when ≥2 of 3 give K1 + K3 and K2 only for a graph-only construct; **refuted** when ≥2 of 3 would build a subset rule on the canvas first.
- **Probe 2 (RQ2).** D1 starts in the form; D2 starts on the canvas; D3 says "it depends on the rule" and names a graph-only construct. H2 is **confirmed** when ≥2 of 3 give D1 or D3.
- **Probe 3 (concept; RQ1 / RQ3).** The reading key for A1 is E36 row 2's sentence: "When a 1m bar closes on BTCUSDT (Main), if a position is open: tighten the stop to 2.0 × ATR(14) from the close. The stop only ever tightens." For A2 the key is E36-D02 :123's fan-out sentence "shared condition {A}, used by {n} actions" plus each action. A0 has no full key (memo SC-13): it is scored on C2 alone — can the participant name which box is the condition and say what `( max )` combines; a fail is the expected result (memo F1) and is recorded as such, never as a participant error. C1 names the trigger; C2 names the condition(s) with their grouping; C3 names the action with its direction; C4 says "only tightens" (or equivalent); C5 says which box is the condition in A2 and that it is shared. **Pass** = C1 + C2 + C3 + C4 (+ C5 for A2). The framing comparison reads out per rule: A-pass vs B-pass per participant; the memo's prediction (A0 fails; A fails for A2; B passes for both) is **confirmed** when ≥2 of 3 pass B and fail A for A2.
- **Probe 4 (comprehension; AC 2).** Per state the pass criteria are (i) which editor can change what, (ii) whether saving and arming are possible, (iii) what happens on switching (memo §5.2). Correct answers from `14`:1858–1862 and `11`:3752–3758: **State 1** — editable in both; save and arm allowed; switching is free. **State 2** — the graph has a part the form cannot edit; the form shows that part as a read-only card and the rest stays editable; saving in the form keeps the part; arming is not blocked by this state; switching shows a confirmation naming the read-only parts. **State 3** — the form and the graph compile to different rules; saving and arming are blocked until one version is chosen; switching does not resolve it. **State 4** — the compile service is unreachable; a draft can be saved; arming is blocked until verified; switching is allowed but unverified. FB per state: FB-2 "my graph is broken / wrong" for state 2; FB-3 "the app will pick the right one" for state 3; FB-4 "my rule is wrong" for state 4. **AC 2's rule:** a state passes when ≥2 of 3 explain it correctly; a failing state's rewrite card is tested in the same session (step 5) and the passing card is recorded; if no card passes, the catalogue's string with the (2c) tooltip ships and the result is recorded against R3.
- **Probe 5 (decision; RQ4).** W1 the participant used the word unprompted for the thing in step 3; W2 picks the set when shown; W3 confuses the word with its competing in-product meaning (edge case, step-up, hyperlink, WebSocket, "blocked"); W4 rejects a word outright. A word **passes** with W1 or W2 and no W3. The set with the most passing words wins the copy layer; a tie goes to the set already used in plan copy (node · port · connection, memo R4).
- **Probe 6 (RQ5).** L1 expects every box to move; L2 expects their hand placement to be kept where possible; L3 names `Ctrl+Z` unprompted; L4 looks for a visible "undo" after the layout. H5 is **confirmed** when ≥2 of 3 give L4 or fail to name L3; **refuted** when ≥2 of 3 give L3 unprompted and reject a visible control.

**Read-out.** One table per probe with the codes per participant, the pass / partial / fail per state and per word, the confirmed / refuted stamp per hypothesis, and the keyboard-first participant's verbatim expectations as a separate block for E37-D04. The memo's R1–R5 are re-stamped from PROPOSED to CONFIRMED or REVISED on #828; nothing in the memo is edited before that comment.
