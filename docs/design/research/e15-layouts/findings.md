## Method note (Agent-delivery adaptation, owner decision 2026-09-25)

The ticket asks for the owner plus 4–6 multi-chart traders (interviews, a card sort, a sync test, a docking probe). One
AI agent and one human owner deliver it; no prototype exists (E15-D02 waits for this report) and `main` has no
workspace, dock or sync UI, only the E15-T01 backend
(`services/api/candleviewer/api/workspaces.py`) and seven Workspace rows in `apps/web/src/keymap/keymap.json`. Per the
Agent-delivery adaptations the sessions become (1) a **heuristic evaluation** of plan, contract and keymap against
Nielsen's ten (H1–H10, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), `05` (§3.1–§3.4, §4, §6.2, §7) and
WCAG 2.2 (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); (2) a **competitive review** (§10); (3) **written instruments**, all runnable
without a prototype; (4) the **owner walkthrough checklist** (§17), ready to post on #205.

**Honest limits.** One evaluator (NN/g recommends three to five:
https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/) read specs and the contract, not a running
interface; no screen reader was run. The v1 population (the owner, 0–5 managers, 0–3 viewers, `10` §0) is too small for
statistics even after a full study. Competitor claims come only from §10's pages (read 2026-10-21); the network refused
TradingView, Bybit and Binance (no workaround tried). **Confidence** is the strength of the basis: **High** = stated in
a locked plan, the contract, keymap or code; **Med** = sound reasoning from cited sources; **Low** = persona inference
or an unobserved expectation, as is every claim about what traders expect. **Pri 0** = can silently change what an order
panel shows or does, or blocks the keyboard path; **Pri 1** = loses work or slows the main path; **Pri 2** = clarity.

## 4. RQ3: is free-form docking worth its cost? Reduce

**Recommendation `(heuristic)`: reduce, not no-go.** **Keep** the split tree, presets, splitter resize and CMP-214's
five dock zones (left, right, top, bottom, tab into), reached by a drag on the header handle, by keyboard move mode and
by the panel menu's "Move to ▸" (SCR-028). **Change:** float becomes a desktop panel-menu command (CMP-216 "pop out";
CMP-215 re-docks), not a sixth drop target (CMP-214 lists no float zone). **Cut for v1:** the browser's "maximised
overlay" (US-LAY-005 and the shipped `BrowserShellAdapter.ts` hide float in the browser with an explanation) and a "view
menu" no screen defines: a hidden panel minimises to a tab (SCR-021) and comes back from it or the panel menu.
**Demote:** drag docking sits below presets and saved layouts; only the header handle advertises it. Fixed slots alone
save little: presets need E15-T02's `packages/dock` tree anyway, and persona-P1's DOM and tape sit on a portrait second
monitor (`10` §2), which needs floating windows. **Evidence:** no usage frequency exists; persona-P1 reuses a saved
workspace (§2); for "arranges once per week" the ticket itself says keep drag docking but demote it; ATAS and MotiveWave
dock by drop targets with a centre tab [AT4, MW5]; Quantower's free panels tab when a header is dropped on another and
bind together [Q4–Q6]; Sierra Chart detaches charts [SC3].

**Cost of the mandatory keyboard equivalent (CMP-214).** No ticket prices it alone. It is half of E15-D04 (5 pts: "the
pointer storyboard and the keyboard move mode are equal deliverables"; its first criterion lists ten operations, each
with a named key, a live preview and an announcement), shares of E15-S02 (5) and E15-T02 (5, whose one `move()` serves
both paths), audited by E15-D15 (3) and E15-Q06 (5). Its fixed part (a visible mode, a target model, key capture ahead
of panel keys, announcements, an exact `Esc` restore) is paid once; each drop target adds a preview, a key path, an
announcement and a test, so float leaves the target set. No competitor page documents keyboard docking (§10): it is a
differentiator to protect (WCAG 2.1.1, 2.5.7), not a cost to cut. **Scope change:** AM-1 (US-LAY-005, SCR-021, SCR-027),
ruled on by the owner before E15-D02 starts (ticket AC3). **Confidence:** Low.

## 5. RQ4: workspaces are documents; layouts are the modes inside them

**5.1 Recommendation `(heuristic)`.** A **workspace is a document**: named, saved, exported and conflict-checked (`22`:
tab order, ETag and `If-Match`, `WorkspaceBundle`); its one mode-like idea, a per-workspace account binding, is out of
E15's scope (E27, E44). A **layout is the mode** inside it: SCR-020's **tab strip shows the workspaces open in this
session**; SCR-011's rail lists them all (open, rename, duplicate, default, reorder); **CMP-073 is the searchable
switcher** both use above 20 workspaces; `[layout v]` and `Ctrl+1..9` switch layouts. Tabs use **manual activation**
(arrows move focus, `Enter` or `Space` opens): a restore may take 3 s (US-LAY-002), and APG advises automatic activation
only "without noticeable latency" (https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tabs/). E15-D15 should add a next/previous
workspace chord. SCR-020 unsubscribes hidden tabs after 30 s, so a panel shown again should read as stale until fresh
data lands (inference; E17-D01 §4 covers live subscriptions). **Evidence:** competitors treat the workspace as a file
with a faster layer inside (§10); one list carries four ARIA patterns (F15). **Confidence:** Low.

**5.2 Unsaved changes (SCR-029): explicit save, never silent loss `(heuristic)`.** Default: `Ctrl+S` saves the **active
layout** (`updateLayout`), `Ctrl+Shift+S` saves it as new; other layouts keep their working copies; workspace name and
tab order save on edit (`updateWorkspace`). Every change is also kept as a **local working copy** (E15-X01's "local
autosave used for crash-restore"), so a crash, a closed window or a lost connection loses nothing, and SCR-020's
"Restored your layout from 14:02" notice gains Save and Revert to saved (proposed); `*` and "unsaved changes" in the
tab's accessible name mark the difference (E15-S03); SCR-029 appears only before the working copy would be discarded
(revert, import over it, delete); `Ctrl+1..9` never prompts; SCR-025's "autosave toggle" stays as an opt-in, off by
default. **Why:** wrong explicit save costs a prompt and loses nothing; wrong autosave writes an accidental change (a
mis-drop, or a one-keystroke preset that "replaces the current layout", SCR-022) over the saved layout within 2 s (`22`
`updateLayout`), and `21` §3.8.1 keeps no history. E15-D04's security note already wants "a drop never silently
overwrites the saved workspace without either an explicit save or a clearly marked dirty state", and R1's exit criteria
want layout state to survive a restart "with zero loss" (`30` §5.3 item 5). Competitors split: explicit save in
NinjaTrader, Sierra Chart, ATAS, Bookmap and Jigsaw, two with versions or backups; Quantower autosaves every five
minutes and keeps `Ctrl+S` (§10). **Confidence:** Low; the guide's block E asks; contract consequence: AM-3.

## 7. Keyboard-dominant and 1280×800 findings, called out separately `(heuristic)`

**Keyboard-dominant** (persona-P1: "every high-frequency action must be reachable by hotkey", `10` §2; a manager's
"keyboard-first operation", `10` §3). (1) The only shipped move binding is `Alt+drag`; the keyboard route is design text
only (CMP-080, CMP-214, SCR-021). (2) Four documented keys move focus, one is shipped (F3), and `Ctrl+Tab` in a browser
leaves the app. (3) The focused panel receives Buy, Sell and Flatten (`16` §8), so naming it is a safety task: the guide
asks "which panel has keyboard focus?" before naming any key. (4) Move mode must capture keys ahead of "valid when:
always" bindings: `Esc` is "Clear ticket" and `Space` re-centres the DOM (`default-keymap.md`). (5) Seven digit families
(`1..9` interval, `1..4` quantity, `Alt+1..9` rail, `Ctrl+1..9` layouts, `Ctrl+Shift+1..3` sync, `Alt+1..4` drawing in
`05` §3.2, `Ctrl+Alt+1..9` SCR-049 cells) put a layout switch one modifier from a group key; explicit save recovers it.

**1280×800** (no persona sits there: `10` §1 gives trading personas a 2560×1440 monitor and the viewer 1920×1080; it is
`16` §8's minimum). (1) E10-D02's TopBar takes 80 px (a 48 px row over the 32 px HealthChips row, as its rework in
PR #4163 has it) and its StatusBar 48, so the canvas is at most 1216×672 px with the nav rail collapsed to 64 px and the
right rail collapsed (its collapsed width is unstated, so 1216 is an upper bound that counts it as 0 px; E15-D02 §3
takes 48, its OQ-A), or 976 px wide with the right rail open (240 px), before
SCR-020's tab strip and panel headers, whose budget E15-D02 sets. (2) A 2×2 then leaves each panel at most 608×336 px;
the first-run default (§2) uses one primary panel and two stacked ones. (3) `10` §3 has a
manager who "prefers larger UI scale (125 %)": as text scale, E10-D02 reflows it (US-SET-007); as OS scaling, it leaves
1024×640 CSS px, below the minimum: `16` §8 shows an "increase window size" advisory with no reflow, while US-LAY-008
collapses panels into tabs under 1280 px; which wins is open (OQ-12).
