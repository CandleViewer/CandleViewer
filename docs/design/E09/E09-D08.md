## 1. Purpose, scope, method, locked inputs

### 1.1 What this spec fixes

Every transition in the auth band, each expressed in the seven shipped motion tokens (`motion.duration.{instant,fast,normal,slow}` = 0 / 100 / 200 / 400 ms, `motion.easing.{standard,decelerate,accelerate}`; `16` §2.6) and on compositor-friendly properties only (opacity and transform; ticket technical notes): the route transitions SCR-001 (login) → SCR-002 (the TOTP two-factor challenge, heading "Two-factor code") → SCR-003 (TOTP enrolment: scan, confirm, recovery codes) / SCR-004 (forced password change) / SCR-010 (the shell) and the backward expired-challenge move; the SCR-003 wizard steps (≤ 200 ms including the focus hand-off); CMP-098's appearance and extend-dismissal; SCR-005 lock-in and unlock-out over a live workspace, with the scrim's arrival and the preservation cue; SCR-006 open / close, the grace-TTL indicator's cadence and the failure feedback; the error regions on SCR-001 / 002 / 004; the busy affordances. Each row carries its reduced-motion variant (`05` §7), the information it carries and its measured cost (§7). The forbidden techniques are listed with measured or cited reasons (§6). SCR-149 is a declared variant of the SCR-005 / SCR-006 chassis (`14`; E09-D06 §1) and inherits the chassis rows; its own admin motion is E42-D04's.

**Out of scope** (verbatim from the ticket): chart-engine animation (pan / zoom easing) — E11 / E12; motion for admin or trading surfaces beyond the step-up chassis they reuse — E42-D04, E30-D04; sound or haptics — not used in this product.

### 1.2 Method (honest)

Desk design against the plan, the merged E09 specs and the shipped runtime, under the agent-delivery adaptations: no participants, no sessions, no quotes. The one thing measured is the prototype of §0, run on **this host** (headless Chromium on WSL2, software rendering, §7.1) at 1280×800 and 2560×1440; its numbers bound the main-thread and compositor-side cost of the transitions themselves and say nothing about the reference machine or the real engine, so AC 1 stays open with §15's protocol. The chart-engine-lead review and the accessibility review the ticket asks for are agent-delivered and recorded as such (§8, §10); the owner's `approved` comment replaces the CDO, chart-engine-lead and accessibility sign-offs.

## 5. Reduced motion: one source, designed per element

### 5.1 The single source and its precedence

The band never asks the browser whether the user prefers reduced motion. It consumes the `16` §4 runtime contract's resolved mode: `data-motion` = `safe | reduce` on `document.documentElement`, written before first paint by `prepaint.ts` as `override === "on" || (override !== "off" && prefers-reduced-motion)` and kept by `store.ts` (`THEME_CHANGE_EVENT` once per committed change). The in-app override `system | on | off` **wins over `prefers-reduced-motion` in both directions** (`16` §4): `off` gives full motion on a machine whose OS says reduce, `on` gives reduce on one that does not. The three entry points — the OS, `Ctrl+Shift+M` (`05` §3.2) and Settings → Accessibility (`apps/web` `a11y-preferences`, `reduced_motion` tri-state) — write the one store; `disable_canvas_animation` is a separate preference about the chart and changes nothing here.

### 5.2 The mid-flight rule

A mode change applies at the next style recalculation **to the transitions that start after it**: the shipped `theme.css` sets every `transition-duration` to `0.01ms !important` under reduce, but CSS Transitions Level 1 §3 keeps a running transition on "the original timing function, duration, and delay" even when those properties change, so a scrim or exit fade already in flight **completes on its own clock** (≤ 200 ms) — it does not jump to its end, and the r2 wording that said so was wrong. A running spinner is a CSS animation, and CSS Animations Level 1 §2 applies changes "as if the animation had those values from when it began", so with `animation-duration: 0.01ms` and `animation-iteration-count: 1` it **stops at that recalculation**. Nothing re-runs; a change back to `safe` mid-flight does not restart a finished transition. No announcement is repeated for a mode change (the shell announces the mode). The bench does not exercise a mid-flight switch; the statement rests on the specifications, not on a measurement.

### 5.3 Coverage tally and the per-element variants

From §3: 11 rows animate under motion-safe and every one has a designed variant — the three scrim fades (rows 2, 13, 17) and the five exits (11, 15, 16, 23, 24) become **instant with the scrim at `opacity.scrim`** (the cards are cuts in both modes already), so the carrier of the preservation cue (the moving canvas under the veil, the kept geometry, the echo) and of the warning (elevation, rule, words, the one announcement) is unchanged; the spinner (rows 5, 39) becomes a static glyph beside the text, with the same slow-path line at 5 s; the toast (row 44) takes its `16` §7 recipe's reduce arm, instant. The forward route moves are cuts in both modes (D10). **No row retains a movement or scale animation under reduce** (AC 2) — none has one under motion-safe either; no information is conveyed only by a removed motion. The 36 rows with no motion in either mode are the same in both modes by construction, which is why the security rows are safe to leave at frame 0.

## 7. The measured cost budget

### 7.1 Host disclosure

Measured 2026-10-21 on this host: `chrome-headless-shell` (Playwright headless-shell build 1247, `HeadlessChrome/155.0.8059.12`), `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` = 28 (28 logical cores), WebGL2 renderer `ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (Subzero) (0x0000C0DE)), SwiftShader driver)` (software rendering, no GPU), WSL2 Linux; viewports 1280×800 and 2560×1440 at DPR 1; 500 ms windows (200 ms for the 0 ms rows, 600 ms for the spinner), 3 reps, 24 backdrop-only frames before each trigger. The runner is `run-bench.mjs` over `playwright-core` 1.64.1 from the workspace's installed packages, launching the Playwright headless-shell build 1247 by explicit `executablePath`; local `file://` pages only, no network. **This is not the `06` §3.1 reference machine:** the host renders in software (SwiftShader) with no GPU, the compositor and the raster run on the CPU, and no real engine worker runs beside the page. The numbers therefore bound the **main-thread cost** (the forced style + layout span) and the **compositor-side cost on this host** (the rAF interval against the control) of the transitions themselves; they cannot show what the real WebGL engine at 2560×1440 pays, so **AC 1 is open** until §15 is run.

**Two runs on the same day, host and binary.** The r3 fold re-specified the chassis entrance and CMP-098, so the four scenarios that describe them — `lock-in-cut`, `stepup-open-cut`, `cmp098-in-modal`, `cmp098-out-modal` — were measured in a **second run** of the same page and runner (2026-10-21; the same `chrome-headless-shell` build 1247, 500 ms windows, 3 reps, 24 backdrop-only frames) and spliced into the committed JSON with `"run": 2`; every first-run row is untouched and the first run's `control` stays the control (the second run's control read 16.8 / 16.7 ms p95 median at 1280×800 and 16.7 / 16.7 at 2560×1440, safe / reduce — the same pace). The r2 rows those four replace in the proposal (`lock-in-translate`, `lock-in-scale`, `stepup-open`, `cmp098-in-fast`, `cmp098-in-normal`, `cmp098-out`) stay in the register as the measured, not-chosen arms (O-1, O-3).

### 7.2 Method

Per scenario and motion mode (`data-motion="safe"` / `"reduce"` on the root, with the shipped `theme.css`), 24 backdrop-only frames precede the trigger; from the trigger frame, every rAF interval, the backdrop's own draw span and the span of a forced style + layout (`offsetHeight`) are recorded for a 500 ms window (200 ms for the 0 ms rows, 600 ms for the spinner); 3 reps; the median of the three p95 intervals is the figure — the flake-tolerance method of `06` §7.4, which is a regression-gate rule and is used here as an absolute pass bar against the control (§7.4) — with the worst p99 and max beside it. A **dropped frame** is an interval over 25 ms (1.5 vsync periods: a missed vsync lands at 33.3 ms); the raw p95 is kept so the 16.6 ms budget can be read directly. The backdrop redraws a full-viewport candle field every frame; the pre-auth route rows run over a static backdrop because those routes carry no chart.

### 7.4 Reading and verdicts

- **Every PROPOSED row passes on this host at both resolutions by the median-of-p95s reading:** the median of the three p95 intervals is the control's vsync pace (16.7–16.8 ms) for every row, and the forced style + layout span is ≤ 0.50 ms p95 (`mask-flip` is exactly 0.50 at 2560×1440 in both modes). **What the reading is:** `06` §7.4 is a CI **regression gate** — a p95 more than 5 % over a stored baseline fails, judged on the median of ≥ 3 repeated runs — and it is borrowed here as an **absolute** pass bar against the backdrop-only control, which is stricter in intent but not what §7.4 was written for; the §15 run keeps §7.4's method and the ticket's absolute number (no frame over 16.6 ms). **Two rows did drop frames, in single reps:** `stepup-fail` (a 0 ms text swap and border change) carried one interval over 25 ms in one rep per resolution — 49.9 ms at 1280×800 under reduce and 33.3 ms at 2560×1440 under motion-safe — and `cmp098-in-modal` carried two in one rep at 2560×1440 under motion-safe (that rep's p95 is 33.3 ms); in every case the style + layout span stayed ≤ 0.6 ms, so the long frames are missed vsyncs on this software-rendered host, not main-thread work; every other rep of those rows and every rep of every other PROPOSED row dropped nothing. Both pass by the median reading and are the rows §15 should watch. **Inferences, named:** the worst single forced span of the run, 2.30 ms, is in `unlock-out` at 1280×800 under motion-safe; the JSON records a per-rep maximum and no frame index, so "frame 0, where the mask lifts and the exit starts" is inferred from the scenario, not read from the data. The 200 ms windows hold about 13 frames, so for those rows (`mask-flip`, `stepup-fail`, `route-cut`, `wizard-step`, `error-region`) the p95 **is** the maximum interval. The `14` SCR-005 "mounts ≤ 100 ms" figure is met by construction on this host: the mount is one frame (the forced layout of the frame that shows the chassis is in the `lock-in-cut` style + layout span) and the entrance adds no layout.
- **The blur control is the row that fails; the other two controls do work the proposal does not:** the blur scrim holds the frame interval at 33.4 ms (median) / 50.1 ms (p95) at 1280×800 and 133.3 / 150.0 ms at 2560×1440 for as long as it is up, in both modes — every frame dropped once the scrim is visible; the height animation forces a layout on every frame of its transition and the shadow animation a paint, which cost nothing measurable on this dozen-node page (F-2, F-3). They are rejected by name in §6 with these numbers. The evidence that transfers is the **scaling**, not the absolute: 2560×1440 has 3.6 × the pixels of 1280×800 and the blur's median frame goes from 33.4 to 133.3 ms — 4 × — the signature of a per-pixel read-back and blur over the whole backdrop on every frame; the absolute milliseconds are SwiftShader's (the raster runs on the CPU) and overstate what a GPU would pay for the same filter, so they are not quoted as the GPU's cost.

## 8. The compositing review (agent-delivered, recorded as such)

The ticket asks for a review with the chart-engine lead confirming the scrim approach is compatible with how the WebGL surfaces composite; under the adaptations it is agent-delivered against `26` and recorded here for the owner's approval.

1. **The scrim is a DOM layer above the canvas on the main thread** (`26` §2: the main thread owns React, the DOM overlays, accessibility and input; everything per-frame runs in the worker on an `OffscreenCanvas`). A fixed-position element with an opacity transition is composited by the browser without touching the worker or the GL context; the engine does not know a lock happened. CMP-098 is the same layer — a second card on the same scrim, cut in at frame 0 — not a separate corner element.
2. **What the worker keeps rendering during the lock:** everything public (chart, ladder, tape) at its normal cadence — E09-D06 §2 item 1 — with `06` §4.4 untouched; the lock sheds nothing and subscribes to nothing. The private panels are DOM and are masked by the host (D1); no engine command is sent. After a revocation or an absolute expiry the socket is closed (`23` §4.4) and the worker simply receives nothing: the last frame is held and E11-D06 D13's stale treatment (the chip, the × 0.85 dim) applies under the veil — again no engine command, and no fade that could read as the lock lifting (rows 7, 28).
3. **Why no `filter` may touch the canvas:** a `backdrop-filter` makes the compositor read back and blur the engine's output every frame for the lifetime of the scrim (F-1 measured: every frame dropped on this host); the `06` §4.3 composite + present budget is 1.6 ms, and the engine's own frame has no slice to lend. The flat 60 % scrim composites as one quad.
4. **The context-loss interaction:** `26` §3.4 handles `webglcontextlost` by stopping the loop, invalidating GPU resources and rebuilding from `DataModel` on restore; E11-D04 §12.2 keeps the last frame and shows the restoring strip (text at 1 Hz, no motion in either mode); E11-D06 D19 makes the restore a cut. Under the lock the strip runs under the scrim unchanged, the lock card stays on top (E09-D06 §2 item 8), and a restore while locked redraws under the veil as a cut — no fade, which would read as the lock lifting.
5. **Input:** the scrim takes every pointer event and the page is `inert` from frame 0 (D6), so `26` §3.11's coalesced input router receives nothing while a chassis is mounted; the engine sees the lock as silence, not as a cancelled gesture.

**Items for E11's engineers to confirm** (each with its cited line): (a) that a fixed DOM layer over the `OffscreenCanvas` host element costs the engine nothing per frame (`26` §2 architecture; `06` §4.3 composite + present ≤ 1.6 ms); (b) that the engine never applies a `filter` or `backdrop-filter` to its own canvas and that the host's chassis never may (F-1; E09-D06 §1); (c) that `inert` on the page while a chassis is mounted silences the input router without emitting a cancel (`26` §3.11); (d) that a context restore under the lock is a cut (E11-D06 D19 / A-3) and the restoring strip has no motion (E11-D04 §12.2); (e) that the `06` §4.4 ladder is never engaged by a lock — the lock changes no engine cadence (E09-D06 §2 item 1); (f) that after a revocation the stale chip and the × 0.85 dim render under the veil with no fade-in on a later restore (E11-D06 D13, D19).
