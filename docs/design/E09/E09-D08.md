## 1. Purpose, scope, method, locked inputs

### 1.1 What this spec fixes

Every transition in the auth band, each expressed in the seven shipped motion tokens (`motion.duration.{instant,fast,normal,slow}` = 0 / 100 / 200 / 400 ms, `motion.easing.{standard,decelerate,accelerate}`; `16` §2.6) and on compositor-friendly properties only (opacity and transform; ticket technical notes): the route transitions SCR-001 (login) → SCR-002 (the TOTP two-factor challenge, heading "Two-factor code") → SCR-003 (TOTP enrolment: scan, confirm, recovery codes) / SCR-004 (forced password change) / SCR-010 (the shell) and the backward expired-challenge move; the SCR-003 wizard steps (≤ 200 ms including the focus hand-off); CMP-098's appearance and extend-dismissal; SCR-005 lock-in and unlock-out over a live workspace, with the scrim's arrival and the preservation cue; SCR-006 open / close, the grace-TTL indicator's cadence and the failure feedback; the error regions on SCR-001 / 002 / 004; the busy affordances. Each row carries its reduced-motion variant (`05` §7), the information it carries and its measured cost (§7). The forbidden techniques are listed with measured or cited reasons (§6). SCR-149 is a declared variant of the SCR-005 / SCR-006 chassis (`14`; E09-D06 §1) and inherits the chassis rows; its own admin motion is E42-D04's.

**Out of scope** (verbatim from the ticket): chart-engine animation (pan / zoom easing) — E11 / E12; motion for admin or trading surfaces beyond the step-up chassis they reuse — E42-D04, E30-D04; sound or haptics — not used in this product.

### 1.2 Method (honest)

Desk design against the plan, the merged E09 specs and the shipped runtime, under the agent-delivery adaptations: no participants, no sessions, no quotes. The one thing measured is the prototype of §0, run on **this host** (headless Chromium on WSL2, software rendering, §7.1) at 1280×800 and 2560×1440; its numbers bound the main-thread and compositor-side cost of the transitions themselves and say nothing about the reference machine or the real engine, so AC 1 stays open with §15's protocol. The chart-engine-lead review and the accessibility review the ticket asks for are agent-delivered and recorded as such (§8, §10); the owner's `approved` comment replaces the CDO, chart-engine-lead and accessibility sign-offs.

## 5. Reduced motion: one source, designed per element

### 5.1 The single source and its precedence

The band never asks the browser whether the user prefers reduced motion. It consumes the `16` §4 runtime contract's resolved mode: `data-motion` = `safe | reduce` on `document.documentElement`, written before first paint by `prepaint.ts` as `override === "on" || (override !== "off" && prefers-reduced-motion)` and kept by `store.ts` (`THEME_CHANGE_EVENT` once per committed change). The in-app override `system | on | off` **wins over `prefers-reduced-motion` in both directions** (`16` §4): `off` gives full motion on a machine whose OS says reduce, `on` gives reduce on one that does not. The three entry points — the OS, `Ctrl+Shift+M` (`05` §3.2) and Settings → Accessibility (`apps/web` `a11y-preferences`, `reduced_motion` tri-state) — write the one store; `disable_canvas_animation` is a separate preference about the chart and changes nothing here.

### 5.2 The mid-flight rule

A mode change applies at the next style recalculation: the shipped `theme.css` sets every `transition-duration` to `0.01ms !important` under reduce, so a scrim or card fade in flight **jumps to its end state** and nothing re-runs; a change back to `safe` mid-flight does not restart a finished transition. No announcement is repeated for a mode change (the shell announces the mode).

### 5.3 Coverage tally and the per-element variants

From §3: 12 rows animate under motion-safe and every one has a designed variant — the six chassis entrances and exits and the three CMP-098 rows become **instant with the scrim at `opacity.scrim` and the card present in frame 0**, so the carrier of the preservation cue (the moving canvas under the veil, the kept geometry, the echo) and of the warning (elevation, rule, glyph, words, the one announcement) is unchanged; the forward route fade becomes a cut (the heading and the focused field carry it); the spinner becomes a static glyph beside the text. **No row retains a movement or scale animation under reduce** (AC 2); no information is conveyed only by a removed motion. The 29 rows with no motion in either mode are the same in both modes by construction, which is why the security rows are safe to leave at frame 0.

## 8. The compositing review (agent-delivered, recorded as such)

The ticket asks for a review with the chart-engine lead confirming the scrim approach is compatible with how the WebGL surfaces composite; under the adaptations it is agent-delivered against `26` and recorded here for the owner's approval.

1. **The scrim is a DOM layer above the canvas on the main thread** (`26` §2: the main thread owns React, the DOM overlays, accessibility and input; everything per-frame runs in the worker on an `OffscreenCanvas`). A fixed-position element with an opacity transition is composited by the browser without touching the worker or the GL context; the engine does not know a lock happened.
2. **What the worker keeps rendering during the lock:** everything public (chart, ladder, tape) at its normal cadence — E09-D06 §2 item 1 — with `06` §4.4 untouched; the lock sheds nothing and subscribes to nothing. The private panels are DOM and are masked by the host (D1); no engine command is sent.
3. **Why no `filter` may touch the canvas:** a `backdrop-filter` makes the compositor read back and blur the engine's output every frame for the lifetime of the scrim (F-1 measured: every frame dropped on this host); the `06` §4.3 composite + present budget is 1.6 ms, and the engine's own frame has no slice to lend. The flat 60 % scrim composites as one quad.
4. **The context-loss interaction:** `26` §3.4 handles `webglcontextlost` by stopping the loop, invalidating GPU resources and rebuilding from `DataModel` on restore; E11-D04 §12.2 keeps the last frame and shows the restoring strip (text at 1 Hz, no motion in either mode); E11-D06 D19 makes the restore a cut. Under the lock the strip runs under the scrim unchanged, the lock card stays on top (E09-D06 §2 item 8), and a restore while locked redraws under the veil as a cut — no fade, which would read as the lock lifting.
5. **Input:** the scrim takes every pointer event and the page is `inert` from frame 0 (D6), so `26` §3.11's coalesced input router receives nothing while a chassis is mounted; the engine sees the lock as silence, not as a cancelled gesture.

**Items for E11's engineers to confirm** (each with its cited line): (a) that a fixed DOM layer over the `OffscreenCanvas` host element costs the engine nothing per frame (`26` §2 architecture; `06` §4.3 composite + present ≤ 1.6 ms); (b) that the engine never applies a `filter` or `backdrop-filter` to its own canvas and that the host's chassis never may (F-1; E09-D06 §1); (c) that `inert` on the page while a chassis is mounted silences the input router without emitting a cancel (`26` §3.11); (d) that a context restore under the lock is a cut (E11-D06 D19 / A-3) and the restoring strip has no motion (E11-D04 §12.2); (e) that the `06` §4.4 ladder is never engaged by a lock — the lock changes no engine cadence (E09-D06 §2 item 1).

## 10. The accessibility self-audit (recorded; E09-D10 runs it)

### 10.1 `05` §10 template, filled for the band's motion

- **Keyboard-only operation:** every transition completes without a pointer; focus lands at frame 0 of each (§9.1); no drag exists; focus is never moved by a background update (permission changes do not move it); the focus ring is in place in the frame focus lands.
- **Screen-reader operation:** every announcement fires at frame 0, before or at the transition's end (§9.2); live text is throttled (`aria-live="off"` countdowns with threshold announcements); no canvas data is involved in the band's own surfaces.
- **Colour-independence:** every state the motion leaves on screen is also text or shape (the mask's words, the alert's glyph and border, the badge's word and glyph).
- **Contrast:** no new colour pair is introduced; the lock card's text on its surface was measured in E09-D06 §9 (13 pairs PASS) and the scrim at `opacity.scrim` over masked panels is not a text pair.
- **Reduced motion:** when the user has `prefers-reduced-motion` enabled (OS or in-app override), every row of §3 that would animate becomes instant with its non-animated carrier and no information is lost (§5.3).
