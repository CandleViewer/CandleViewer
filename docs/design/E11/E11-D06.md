## 1. Purpose, scope and method

### 1.1 What this spec fixes

The motion of the SCR-030 chassis, element by element, with a duration, an easing and a designed reduced-motion variant per row (§7): the pan / zoom momentum model with its constants, release rule, clamps and cancellations (§3); the programmatic viewport transitions — double-click resets, `fit` / `›` / `End` / `Home` / `Ctrl+Home`, the lock-badge restore and the go-to-date landing — with the rule that cuts a jump that is too far to animate, stated as numbers (§4); the crosshair's no-easing rule, its fades and the magnet snap (§5); the CMP-226 countdown's 1 Hz tick and warn flip, the stale dimming, the LOD step and the aggregation chip, the splitter and the collapsed pane, the context-loss "restoring…" strip, the skeleton shimmer and the follow-live shift (§6); the safety rows — the environment badge and the replay frame never transition, the crosshair position never lags (§8); the single source of the motion mode and what reduced motion means per element (§9); the frame-budget register and the shedding order (§10); the owner's feel-validation protocol, walkthrough and reduced-motion script (§11); a heuristic evaluation (§12); traceability, contradictions, contract gaps and open questions (§13); the sign-off checklists (§14); the catalogue amendments (§15); the hand-offs (§16). Out of scope, per #311: drawing-tool and order-ticket motion (E14, E31), application-shell transitions (E05 / E10 — this spec only confirms the shell recipes the chassis inherits, D15), the implementation (E11-S04 and the consuming stories), the heatmap decay / trail (E19, `05` §7 names it; the `heatmapFade` flag is mapped in D21 and nothing else).

### 1.2 Method (honest)

Desk design under the agent-delivery adaptations (#311): no participants, no sessions, no prototype — **no interactive build of the engine exists on `main` at `5f86cbbb8f5b18ab1d8cb7f9849fe33cb5a6fa8b`** (E11-D01 Q1-f / O2; E11-D02 §14: the E06 spikes are Node benches with modelled GPU cost and no pointer input; `packages/chart-engine/bench/scenes/viewport.mjs` carries the §3.5 model as `MOMENTUM_TAU_MS = 120` and `MOMENTUM_STOP_PX_PER_MS = 0.02` but has no input, no canvas and no frame loop). So the ticket's "validated by feel against the E06 build with the owner" **could not be run**: every constant below is **PROPOSED**, each choice beyond the plan's own numbers is labelled `(heuristic)`, and §11 is the protocol the owner runs — numbered trials, the ranges to try, the recording sheet and the pass criteria — after which the final constants and any deviation from `26` §3.5 are recorded with their reason (ticket AC 2). The arithmetic is computed, not estimated: `docs/design/_tools/e11_d06_motion_audit.py` produces every worked number and contrast ratio quoted here. Nothing is claimed as validated; nothing is faked.

## 2. Decisions at a glance

Each row is `(heuristic)` unless it restates a plan number; **every numeric value below is PROPOSED until the owner's feel run (§11) records it with evidence.** Detail and rationale in §3–§10; consumers and ids in §13.1.

| #   | Decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Section |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| D1  | **Momentum is an exponential coast integrated against elapsed time, never a per-frame multiplier:** τ = 120 ms (`26` §3.5, PROPOSED; range for the run 80–200 ms); per frame `x += v·τ·(1 − e^{−dt/τ})`, `v ·= e^{−dt/τ}`; the rest position is the closed form `x∞ = x₀ + v₀·τ` and is identical at 30, 60 and 144 fps and with dropped frames (§3.3). The coast is **time-axis only**: the vertical velocity is zeroed at release, so a price pan (a locked scale only) stops where pointer-up left it; under auto-scale the price range refits every frame of the coast, under a locked range it is held (§3.1).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | §3      |
| D2  | **Termination** when `\|v\| < 0.02 px/ms` (`26` §3.5); the terminating frame applies the analytic remainder `v·τ` (< 2.4 px), so the rest is bit-for-bit `x∞`; no snap-to-bar or device-pixel snap afterwards — nothing moves after the user sees it stop. The trade-off is stated: that remainder is up to 6.7 × the terminating frame's own step at 60 fps (3.1 × at 30, 16.8 × at 144) — one faster frame traded for a rest with nothing after it; the distance-based rule `abs(v)·τ < 0.5 px` (remainder under half a pixel, 188 ms more tail) is T2's second arm (§3.2).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | §3.2    |
| D3  | **Release velocity and the no-fling floor:** `v₀` = the exponentially weighted velocity of the coalesced pointer samples in the last 50 ms before pointer-up (E11-S04); no coast when the pointer was still for > 100 ms before release or `\|v₀\| < 0.3 px/ms` (PROPOSED) — a careful drag ends exactly where the last pointer event put it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | §3.1    |
| D4  | **Maximum fling distance 1.5 viewport widths** (PROPOSED; run 1 / 1.5 / 2 W), enforced as a velocity clamp `\|v₀\| ≤ D_max / τ` (15 px/ms on a 1,200 px plot); the coast obeys every clamp a drag obeys — the bar-spacing clamp, E11-S04's right-margin limit, the loaded edge's loading band — with a hard stop and no bounce — by the same reasoning as E11-D01 Q1-i's zoom-clamp ruling; reaching a clamp zeroes the velocity.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | §3.1    |
| D5  | **Cancellation:** a pointer-down, a wheel event, any key that moves the viewport or the cursor, a programmatic move (`setViewport`, go-to-date, `fit`, `End`), a scale-mode change, the data table opening (host `suspend()`), a context loss, a resize, a motion-mode change to `reduce` and an incoming sync-group range each stop a coast **or a transition in flight where it is** (velocity zeroed, no completion jump; the user's input is the authority on where the chart is — only a motion-mode change to `reduce` sends a transition to its target, D21). Momentum applies to pointer and touch pans only (drag, `Space`+drag, two-finger drag) — never to wheel, pinch, keyboard or programmatic moves; in a sync group the member that received the gesture integrates and the others follow the published range.                                                                                             | §3.1    |
| D6  | **The cut rule** (one rule for every programmatic transition): animate only if the pan distance between the current and target viewport centres is ≤ **3 current viewport widths** (the ticket's number), the bar-spacing ratio `max(s_t/s_c, s_c/s_t)` ≤ **4**, and — under a locked range only; skipped under auto-scale, where the target's range is the refit of the target window (§4.3) — the price-range ratio ≤ 4 with overlapping ranges; otherwise **cut** to the target in one frame and let the axis labels and the announcement orient. ## 3. Momentum (D1–D5)

### 3.1 The model, the release and the clamps

The pan coasts after a pointer or touch release with velocity `v₀` and decays exponentially: `v(t) = v₀·e^{−t/τ}`, `x(t) = x₀ + v₀·τ·(1 − e^{−t/τ})`, so the coast travels exactly `v₀·τ` and the time constant is the whole feel: at τ = 120 ms the coast is 63 % done after 120 ms and 95 % done after 360 ms. Everything else is a rule around that curve:

- **Which gestures coast** `(heuristic)`: a plot drag (pointer, pen, `Space`+drag) and a two-finger touch drag — the gestures whose release has a velocity. Never a wheel or trackpad scroll (the OS already applies its own inertia to trackpad deltas; a second coast on top would double it), never a pinch (zoom inertia overshoots the level the trader meant to read), never a keyboard step or a programmatic move (D8). In a sync group the chart that received the gesture integrates the coast and publishes its range once per frame (`26` §3.5 single-phase propagation); the members apply it and never integrate their own.
- **Which axis coasts** `(heuristic)`: the **time axis only**. `26` §3.11's drag row pans "time (and price if the price scale is unlocked) with momentum", and D1 / row 1 integrate bar-index only: the **vertical velocity is zeroed at release**, so a price pan — possible only under a locked scale, since a vertical plot drag is inert while auto-scale is on (E11-D03 §1.3) — stops exactly where pointer-up left it. A vertical coast would move the price under a resting pointer, under every order line and under every drawing handle with no browse to carry; the time coast is the browse. **Auto-scale during the coast:** with auto-scale on, the price range **refits every frame** of the coast — `26` §3.5's own rule (recomputed when the visible bar range changes, throttled to once per frame; the public Lightweight Charts `autoScale` option, "automatically adjusts a price scale to fit the visible range of data", is the same contract) — the same computation as a frame of a held pan, so a coast costs what a drag costs (§10). With a **locked (manual) range the range does not change** during the coast. Whether the refit should run per frame or once at rest is a feel question — trial T11 and O-11 (PROPOSED: per frame, because a range that waits for the rest clips the bars that coast in from the edge).
- **What the chassis does while the chart coasts** `(heuristic)`: the crosshair stays **hidden through the coast** and reappears at 0 ms, at the pointer's position, in the frame the coast ends — its terminating frame, or a cancellation that is not itself a drag or a wheel burst — or at the next `pointermove`, whichever comes first (row 9, §5.2; PROPOSED): the coast is the drag's continuation, so E11-D02 §7.3's rule — hidden during a drag or a wheel burst, back "on the first idle frame" because "the pointer is navigating, not reading" — covers it literally (a coasting chart has no idle frame until it rests), and a line re-resolved per frame over bars passing at up to 8 px/ms would churn its pills and the legend through tens of bars in half a second on the one instrument meant for reading; trial T9's third arm puts the alternative (back at pointer-up, re-resolved per frame) beside it. The pills and the legend re-resolve in the frame it reappears; a D7 transition in flight under a resting pointer follows the same rule `(heuristic)`. The mirror's `aria-current` row and the D10 column never follow a hover (E06-D03: the pointer is hover and changes neither; a click sets the selected cursor, which then rides its bar through the coast), so the live region says nothing. The cluster's `Live` flips to `Paused at hh:mm` in the frame the latest bar leaves the screen, exactly as under a drag (E11-D01 Q2-c; a state flip, D15), and nothing flips it back when a coast ends with the latest bar on screen — follow resumes only by `End`, `›`, the toggle or the plot double-click; while follow is still on, a bar that opens mid-coast applies its one-bar shift (row 7; Q1-i(3): the current `rightOffset` is preserved as bars arrive) in the same frame as the coast's step.
- **The release velocity** (E11-S04 Technical notes, kept): the exponentially weighted mean of the coalesced pointer samples in the last **50 ms** before pointer-up, not a two-point difference (noisy at high sample rates; flicks would feel inconsistent). A pointer that was **still for > 100 ms** before release has `v₀ = 0` — a trader who drags, stops to read, then lets go gets no coast `(heuristic)`.
- **The no-fling floor** `(heuristic)`: `|v₀| < 0.3 px/ms` starts no coast; the chart stays exactly where the last pointer event put it. Without a floor a slow, careful positioning drag would drift 1–30 px after release — the single most-reported "slippery" feel. Trial T3 tries 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.6 px/ms.
- **The maximum fling** `(heuristic)`: `D_max = 1.5 W` (W = the plot width; 1,800 px on the 1,200 px reference plot), enforced before integration as `|v₀| ← min(|v₀|, D_max / τ)` — 15 px/ms on a 1,200 px plot, which only ever bites on a trackpad or touch flick (a hard mouse flick is 5–10 px/ms). A coast beyond a viewport and a half carries no spatial cue (the same reasoning as the cut rule, D6) and lands the trader somewhere they did not point at. Trial T4 tries 1 / 1.5 / 2 W.
- **Clamps**: the coast obeys every clamp a drag obeys — the bar-spacing clamp is not relevant to a pan, but the right-margin limit E11-S04 defines and the loaded edge are: the coast may run **into** the loading band (E11-D01 Q1-i: the band is drawn left of the oldest loaded bar and the time axis keeps ticking across it; paging is triggered by `visibleRangeNeedsData` as for a drag) but stops hard at the right-margin limit with **no bounce** — by the same reasoning as E11-D01 Q1-i's zoom-clamp ruling (a bounce is motion without information) — reaching a clamp sets `v = 0` so the coast does not keep pushing against the edge and the `viewportChanged` stream goes quiet (the loop parks, `26` §3.2 rule 5).
- **Cancellation** (D5): the list in §2 — the engine checks it before integrating the frame, so a pointer-down in the same frame as the coast's next step wins. A cancelled coast — or a cancelled D7 transition, which the same list stops (H3) — stops **where it is**: it never jumps to `x∞` or to the transition's target, because the user's new input is the authority on where the chart is; the one exception is the motion-mode change to `reduce`, which sends a transition in flight to its target (D21) because that change asks for the destination without the motion. The motion-mode change to `reduce` is on the list so a coast in flight when the user presses `Ctrl+Shift+M` stops at once.

### 3.2 Termination and the rest position (D2)

The coast ends in the first frame where `|v| < 0.02 px/ms` (`26` §3.5): below that the chart moves under a third of a pixel per frame, invisible. At that instant the analytic remainder is `v·τ < 0.02 × 120 = 2.4 px`; the terminating frame **applies it** (`x ← x + v·τ`, then `v ← 0`), so the rest position is exactly `x∞ = x₀ + v₀·τ` and is the same number on every machine — which makes "the chart comes to rest in the same place at 30 and 60 fps" a unit-testable property (E11-S04 unit list already names "momentum integration with `TestClock` (including dropped-frame equivalence)"). The trade-off is stated, not hidden: the applied remainder — up to `v_term·τ` = 2.4 CSS px (3 device px at 125 %) — is a one-frame step of 3.1 / 6.7 / 16.8 × the terminating frame's own step at 30 / 60 / 144 fps (the audit's §1 computes `k / (1 − k)`), a velocity spike in the frame that is meant to be the stop, traded for a rest with nothing moving after it. The alternative is a **distance-based rule**, `abs(v)·τ < 0.5 px`: the remainder stays under half a pixel (0.40–0.49 px in the audit) at the cost of **188.2 ms** more sub-pixel tail at every release velocity (`τ·ln(0.02 / 0.00417)`; +5 or 6 / +11 / +27 frames at 30 / 60 / 144 fps; 657.7 / 789.5 / 907.2 ms to rest for the three releases). Both are arms of trial T2; the velocity rule stays PROPOSED because `26` §3.5 names it, and the run decides by whether the stop is visible (T2's "abrupt stop" column). After termination nothing snaps: not to a bar boundary, not to a device pixel. A snap would move the chart after the user saw it stop, and GL draws fractional bar offsets correctly; the crosshair snaps to the bar centre anyway (`26` §3.11).

### 3.3 Framerate independence, worked (the #311 technical note made numeric)

The integration must use elapsed time with the exact exponential — `k = e^{−dt/τ}`, `x += v·τ·(1 − k)`, `v ·= k` — because the exponential has the semigroup property: two frames of 16.7 ms and one of 33.3 ms produce the same `x` and `v`, so dropped frames change nothing but the number of frames drawn. The mistake #311 warns of is a **per-frame multiplier tuned at one frame rate** (`k₆₀ = e^{−16.67/120} = 0.8703`, applied every frame whatever `dt` was): it decays per frame, not per millisecond, so the coast lasts twice as long at 30 fps and under half as long at 144 fps. The audit computes both for three releases on a 1,200 px plot at τ = 120 ms:

| release | `v₀` px/ms | `x∞ = v₀·τ` | `t_stop = τ·ln(v₀/0.02)` | exact, 30 fps: frames / short of `x∞` before the remainder | 60 fps       | 144 fps       | 60 fps with every 3rd frame dropped | the per-frame-multiplier mistake, rest at 30 / 60 / 144 fps |
| ------- | ---------- | ----------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| gentle  | 1.0        | 120.0 px    | 469.4 ms                 | 15 / 1.86 px                                               | 29 / 2.14 px | 68 / 2.35 px  | 22 / 2.14 px                        | 252.5 / 126.2 / 52.6 px                                     |
| brisk   | 3.0        | 360.0 px    | 601.3 ms                 | 19 / 1.84 px                                               | 37 / 2.11 px | 87 / 2.34 px  | 28 / 2.11 px                        | 766.6 / 383.3 / 159.7 px                                    |
| hard    | 8.0        | 960.0 px    | 719.0 ms                 | 22 / 2.13 px                                               | 44 / 2.13 px | 104 / 2.34 px | 33 / 2.13 px                        | 2,051.9 / 1,025.9 / 427.5 px                                |

Reading the table: with the exact method the terminating frame is short of `x∞` only by the sub-2.4 px remainder (it varies with where the frame boundary falls — 1.84 to 2.35 px here), and D2 applies that remainder, so the rest position is **identical at every frame rate and with dropped frames**: 120.0, 360.0 and 960.0 px. With the multiplier mistake the hard flick travels 2,052 px at 30 fps and 428 px at 144 fps — a chart that lands in a different decade of bars depending on how busy the machine was, which is the correctness defect the ticket names. E11-S04's acceptance: the same release at 30, 60 and 144 fps and with the 1-in-3 dropped-frame pattern lands on the same bar index to within **1e-6 bar — the unit-test tolerance** (unit, `TestClock`); the owner's trial T8 repeats it by hand, where **0.01 bar is the hand-check tolerance** (the harness prints the landing index).

### 3.4 Why τ is validated by feel and what the run decides

τ = 120 ms is `26` §3.5's proposal and the only number in this section the plan fixes; it is kept as the proposed default because nothing on `main` contradicts it (E11-D01 Q1-f: "E11-D06 proceeds on τ = 120 ms"). The owner's run (§11, T1) sweeps 80 / 100 / 120 / 150 / 200 ms: below ~100 ms a coast reads as a stop with a stumble, above ~150 ms as a slide the trader has to catch `(heuristic — a reading of the curve, not an observation)`. Whatever the run records becomes the number, and if it differs from 120 ms the deviation and its reason are recorded here and amended into `26` §3.5 (A-1), per #311 AC 2.

## 4. Programmatic transitions and the cut rule (D6–D8)

### 4.1 One rule for every jump

A **programmatic transition** is any viewport change the user did not drive continuously: the plot double-click (auto-scale + latest bar, E11-D01 Q1-d), `End` / the `›` segment (latest bar at the default right offset), `fit` / `Home` on the time strip (fit the loaded bars), `Ctrl+Home` / the time-axis double-click (default spacing), the price-axis double-click / `Home` / the lock badge (auto-fit), the go-to-date landing (E11-D03 §7.5), a keyboard `Home` / `End` of the data cursor, and a sync-group member following another chart's jump. The animation is a **spatial cue** — it says which way and how far — and that cue has a range: beyond it the motion is a delay with nothing to read. The rule, `(heuristic)` in its numbers:

> **Animate only if all three hold, else cut:** (1) the pan distance between the current and the target viewport centres, in **current** viewport widths, is **≤ 3 W**; (2) the bar-spacing ratio `max(s_target / s_current, s_current / s_target)` is **≤ 4**; (3) under a **locked range only** — skipped under auto-scale, where the target's range is the refit of the target window and not a jump to measure (§4.3) — the price-range ratio is ≤ 4 **and** the ranges overlap. A **cut** is one frame to the target; the time-axis labels (E11-D02 D4), the `Paused at hh:mm` / `Live` word on the cluster, the go-to-date marker and the announcement do the orienting.

Why three tests and not one: a pan across many viewports and a zoom across many octaves are both "too far", but in different units — a 25× `fit` has zero pan distance. Why 4 for the ratio `(heuristic)`: two octaves of spacing is the most a 200–400 ms zoom can cross while the bars at both ends are still recognisably the same objects; above it the intermediate frames show a chart that is neither, and the LOD hysteresis band is crossed twice (12 → 10.2 → 8.5 px) in a few frames. Bounds are inclusive so the published number is the last value that animates. Worked (audit §2, 1,200 px plot at 6 px/bar = 200 bars per viewport): a **30,000-bar go-to-date jump is 150 W → cut** (#311 AC 4); 500 bars = 2.5 W → animate at `slow`; 600 bars = 3.0 W → animate (inclusive); 601 bars = 3.005 W → cut; `fit` from 200 visible to 5,000 loaded bars = ratio 25 → cut; `Ctrl+Home` from 3 to 12 px/bar = ratio 4 → animate; from 0.5 to 12 px/bar = ratio 24 → cut; a plot double-click from one viewport away → animate at `normal`.

The engine owns the rule: it alone knows `W`, the spacing and the price ranges, so the host asks for a transition and the engine decides animate-or-cut (contract gap G-3 — `setViewport` today is immediate; E11-S04 adds the transition option). The announcement for a jump is composed from the **target** (E11-D03 §7.5's one sentence) and fires when the transition starts, never when it ends: a screen-reader user hears the landing while a sighted user watches it, and a cut and an animation announce identically.

### 4.2 Duration and easing (D7)

| Travel (after the cut rule)                            | Duration token                  | Easing token             | Interpolation space                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| pan ≤ 1 W (and any pure scale change within the ratio) | `motion.duration.normal` 200 ms | `motion.easing.standard` | pan in bar-index space (bars move at a constant screen speed); zoom in `log(barSpacing)` (a constant zoom factor per frame) about an anchor — the latest bar's x under follow, else the viewport centre; price linearly in the scale's own space (log space in log mode), or under auto-scale the range refit every frame from the interpolated window; all three interpolated together from one eased `u ∈ [0, 1]` |
| 1 W < pan ≤ 3 W                                        | `motion.duration.slow` 400 ms   | `motion.easing.standard` | as above                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| beyond the rule                                        | `motion.duration.instant` 0 ms  | —                        | cut                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

Two token steps and not a continuous distance-to-duration function `(heuristic)`: the tokens are the only durations the design system has, a trader learns two speeds, and the two steps keep the peak speed bounded. The audit's kinematics (§3 of its output) explain the choices with numbers at 60 fps: `standard` covers 10 % of the travel in the first frame (`decelerate`: 41 % — a cut followed by a settle, which reads as a glitch rather than a cue), and the peak per-frame displacement is 368 px for 1 W at `normal` and 562 px for 3 W at `slow`; the rejected 3 W at `normal` would peak at 1,104 px per frame — a full viewport width in one frame, which is a cut with extra steps. For scale, the hardest momentum fling (8 px/ms) moves 124 px in its first 60 fps frame under the exact model (the linear bound `v₀·dt` would be 133 px). The first-frame fraction depends on the frame rate (standard: 0.41 / 0.10 / 0.01 at 30 / 60 / 144 fps) but the end position does not — transitions, like momentum, integrate elapsed time (`u = t / duration`, clamped), so a dropped frame never changes where a jump lands (`26` §3.2 rule 2). The objection that `decelerate` (0, 0, 0, 1) is a degenerate ease-out — a vertical start tangent — and that reference systems arrive with a proper ease-out is answered with a third arm rather than a ruling: Material 3's `emphasized-decelerate`, `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)` (material-web `tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss`, the public M3 token source; the same file's `easing-standard` (0.2, 0, 0, 1) and `easing-standard-decelerate` (0, 0, 0, 1) are exactly this design system's two tokens). It is not a token, so it is a change request to E11-D05 (A-16), and the audit computes it beside the other two: by the first-frame metric it is the most front-loaded of the three (58 % of the travel in the first 60 fps frame at `normal`, 74 % at 30 fps; peaks 691 / 1,450 / 2,074 px) — a reading, not a verdict. Trial T7 compares `standard`, `decelerate` and the M3 curve at 150 / 200 / 300 ms; the owner may well prefer the snap of an ease-out for `End` — recorded as the alternatives, not dismissed.

**Price during a transition, and the zoom anchor** `(heuristic)`: under auto-scale an animated D7 transition **refits the price range every frame** from the interpolated time window — the same per-frame computation as the coast and a held pan (`26` §3.5), so the bars never leave the pane mid-flight; under a locked range a time-only jump leaves the range unchanged, and the price-axis auto-fit (the lock badge, `Home`, the gutter double-click) is the one transition that interpolates price, in the scale's own space. A **cut is one frame for the time axis and the price range together** — never time first and the refit a frame later, which would show one frame of bars outside the pane. The `log(barSpacing)` interpolation needs an anchor, because interpolating the window's centre moves the latest bar under follow: the anchor is **the latest bar's x under follow, else the viewport centre**, held fixed for the whole transition (the same invariant as the wheel's cursor anchor, `26` §3.5).

### 4.3 The go-to-date landing (E11-D03 §7.5) and the reset-class jumps

Go-to-date: the dialog closes first, focus returns to the invoking control, then the engine runs the D6 test on the target viewport (the target bar centred at the **current** spacing — a pan only, so test (2) always passes; test (3) applies only under a locked range and is skipped under auto-scale, where the target's range is the refit of the target window and not a jump to measure — so the pan distance decides); a target outside the loaded pages shows the loading band and the view changes only when the page lands (E11-D03 §8 — nothing jumps twice), so the transition starts from the landed page's position and is almost always a cut (anything paged in is far). The marker and its pill, the cursor column and the `Paused at …` word appear on the **first frame** of the transition, at their target positions, not at its end `(heuristic — the destination is the information; the motion is the cue)`. `End` / `›` / the plot double-click back to live: a pan of the current distance to the live edge, usually ≤ 3 W after a short browse (animate) and > 3 W after a deep one (cut); the cluster's word flips to `Live` on the first frame. If a bar opens during the `End` transition the live edge has moved: the target is re-aimed by one bar spacing on that frame — same end time, no restart, no second announcement — so the latest bar lands at the default right offset (E11-D01 Q1-i(3)), and follow resumes on the final frame as if the bar had opened then. `fit`: a ratio test — almost always a cut on a 5,000-bar series. The price-axis auto-fit (double-click, `Home`, the lock badge): a price-range change with no pan; the ratio test decides (a locked scale dragged to twice its range animates back at `normal`; one dragged far past overlap cuts). During a transition the axis ladders (E11-D02 D4 / D5) are **recomputed on the final frame only** and the labels slide with the scale meanwhile — the same no-flicker rule as D18.

### 4.4 What never animates (D8)

Continuous input is applied in the frame it arrives, exactly as `26` §3.11 states (events coalesced and applied once per frame): the wheel zoom about the cursor, the trackpad pinch about the centroid, `Shift+wheel` / `deltaX` pans, the axis drags and the axis wheels. **No smoothing is added to wheel zoom** `(heuristic)`: a smoothed wheel makes the cursor-anchored point drift during the smoothing and makes a quick two-notch zoom overshoot — What can be sourced: TradingView's Lightweight Charts exposes the wheel as a boolean with no smoothing option (`handleScale.mouseWheel`, default `true`; `handleScroll.mouseWheel`, default `true`) and keeps kinetic scrolling off for the mouse (`kineticScroll.mouse` default `false`, `.touch` default `true`) — public API docs; Sierra Chart's General Settings document the scroll wheel as changing bar spacing or scrolling the chart, with no smoothing setting on the page — public docs, "Scroll Wheel"; that Bookmap applies the wheel per notch is `(heuristic, unsourced observation — its public page says only that the wheel zooms)`. Every keyboard **step** is one frame: the time-axis `←/→` 10 % and `Shift` one width, `+/−` × 1.1 and `Shift` × 1.25, the price-axis `↑/↓` 10 % and `Shift` 25 %, `PageUp/PageDown`, the data cursor's `←/→` / `Shift` ×10 / `↑/↓`, the splitter's 2 % / 10 %. Keys auto-repeat at the OS rate (~30 Hz): an eased step would lag the repeat and smear, and the keyboard path is the reduced-motion path by design (#311 a11y note) — only the keyboard **jumps** (`Home`, `End`, `Ctrl+Home`, cluster `Enter`) go through D6 / D7, because for a sighted keyboard user the spatial cue is as useful as for a pointer user, and under reduce they cut like everything else. Follow-live's shift when a new bar opens is one frame (Lightweight Charts shifts the visible range "by the number of new bars when new data is added" — `shiftVisibleRangeOnNewBar`, default `true`, no animation option, public docs; that Sierra Chart and ATAS step likewise is `(heuristic, unsourced observation)`; an animated one-bar glide every minute is motion for its own sake and would need a pause control past 5 s, `05` §7 item 2 — the `auto` toggle already is one).

## 5. The crosshair (D9–D11)

### 5.1 Position: no easing, no interpolation, no lag

The crosshair is a reading instrument: its vertical line names the bar and its horizontal line the price the trader is about to act on, so it is placed from the **latest pointer sample of the frame** and drawn where that sample is — never eased toward it, never interpolated between frames, never smoothed `(#311 AC 3; a lagging crosshair is a correctness defect)`. Its axis pills are GL text in the axis batch (E11-D02 ORCH-13), positioned in the same frame as the lines, so pills and lines cannot drift apart by the one-frame worker skew; only the DOM tooltip (CMP-197) may trail by a frame, and it is `aria-hidden` and not the readout of record (the mirror and the legend values are). Budget: input ≤ 1 ms per frame (`06` §4.3), input-to-pixel ≤ 1 frame (US-CHART-006 NFR — the stricter of the two latency numbers, C-10). The same rule holds for the remote crosshair of a linked chart (E11-D01 Q3-c): it is drawn at the published time with no easing; if a sync frame is late the line is late, never interpolated to look on time.

### 5.2 Appearance and fade (the only crosshair timings)

Why appearance is 0 ms and only the disappearance fades `(heuristic)`: the instrument is needed the moment the pointer arrives — a fade-in delays the first reading by 100 ms and shows a half-visible line over the price the trader is checking; a fade-out, by contrast, only softens the blink when the pointer skims the plot edge, and the frozen position guarantees the fading line never points at a price the pointer is not on. The fade is one alpha uniform on the foreground batch (no extra pass, §10). **Why the burst end is a number** `(heuristic)`: E11-D02 §7.3's "first idle frame" has no terminal event for a wheel — read literally (the first frame with no accumulated wheel delta, `26` §3.11) a slow notch-by-notch zoom at 100–150 ms per notch would blink the crosshair in and out on every notch, which is the flicker the ticket's "designed, not improvised" rule exists to prevent and a number E11-S07 would otherwise pick alone. 150 ms holds the crosshair hidden through a slow notch sequence and returns it within one notch interval of the last; a `pointermove` returns it at once, because a moving pointer is a trader reading, not navigating (the TradingView convention). Trial T9 tries 150 / 250 ms. The same rule covers the coast `(heuristic)`: E11-D02 §7.3 brings the crosshair back "on the first idle frame", and a chart coasting under a resting pointer has none until it rests — the coast is the drag's continuation — so the crosshair stays hidden until the chart rests, and the next `pointermove` returns it at once; a crosshair that returned at pointer-up and re-resolved per frame would churn its pills and the legend through tens of bars in half a second. T9's third arm puts that alternative beside the proposal. The keyboard cursor column (E06-D03 D10) is not part of this fade: it persists while the root has focus and is removed instantly when focus leaves.

### 5.3 Magnet snap (D10)

With `magnetMode` `weak` or `strong` (E11-S07; off by default, E11-D01 D3), the horizontal line and the readout jump to the nearest OHLC value: **in the same frame** the pointer enters the snap radius, with no interpolation from the free price to the snapped one, and the pill gains the OHLC letter and the 12 px magnet glyph in that frame (E11-D02 §7.3) — on board 04 the worked example reads `64,011.0` free, `H 64,120.0 ⌖` snapped to the high, `64,013.5` after the release. Leaving the radius in `weak` mode releases the snap in the same frame, with a **2 px release hysteresis** (enter at `r`, release at `r + 2 px`, PROPOSED) so a pointer resting on the boundary cannot make the line oscillate between the free and the snapped price — the same dead-band logic as the LOD thresholds, at pointer scale. The radius itself is not this spec's number: E11-S07 writes 8 px and E11-D01 D3 / Q3-b proposes 12 px pending E14-D01 #204 (O-7); the timing is radius-independent. Identical in both motion modes — a snap is a discrete state change, not an animation, and the letter in the pill (and in the tooltip, and in the cursor announcement) carries it.

### 5.4 The keyboard cursor column and the other keyboard-driven positions (D11)

E06-D03 D10 lets the focus-ring column "ease ≤ `motion.duration.fast`" under motion-safe; E11-D02 §12 carried that as the default ("eases ≤ fast → snaps"). This spec decides **it never eases**, in either mode: the column marks the bar the readout, the mirror's `aria-current` row and the live region name in the same frame (E06-D03 D5 announces the first press at once), so a column still travelling between two bars while the summary already names the target is the lagging crosshair in keyboard form; and under auto-repeat (`repeatWindow` 500 ms, OS repeat ~30 Hz) a 100 ms ease would spend a fifth of every step — or, at the OS repeat rate, the whole of it — between bars. The same decision closes E11-D02 §7.1's open question for the price-axis keyboard rescale (a ladder that re-computes on every eased frame flickers its labels; one frame, as E11-D03 §8 already rules for the scale mode) and E11-D03 §8's "may ease ≤ fast" for the bar-spacing presets and the `fit` step (one frame). What stays visible across a keyboard move is the column's halo and the chart ring — persistence, not motion.

The information is the chip's word, age and last-good timestamp (E17-D01 D6: opacity alone never signals state); the dim is reinforcement, which is why the chip is **always** instant and only the dim has a pair. The entry ramp is `normal` rather than `fast` `(heuristic)`: a 100 ms dim on data the trader is reading looks like a flicker of the display; 200 ms reads as the chart "stepping back". The exit ramp matches it rather than snapping `(heuristic)`: the live frames are already drawn under the lifting dim — nothing is withheld — and an instant brightening on a chart the trader is watching reads as a tick event. Under reduce both are instant: the dimmed state still exists (the ticket's "static dimmed treatment"), it just arrives in one frame. The ramp is the same uniform the state entry writes (E11-D02 D16, §13 register: 0 passes, one upload per frame while ramping — 12 frames — then none); the dim never follows the age, never pulses, and never flickers at the threshold because the **state** debounces (E17-D01 §4: exits follow the exit column; "warn fast, clear slow"), not the fade. The disconnected scrim and strip (E11-D02 §10) are not a motion: they appear at 0 ms in both modes.

### 6.3 LOD step and the aggregation chip (D14)

A candle LOD step (L0 → L1 at 10.2 px, L1 → L0 at 12 px; L1 → L2 at 8.5 px, L2 → L1 at 10 px — E06-D01 §2's asymmetric band; footprint L0–L3 likewise) is **one frame, in both modes**. No crossfade, by decision and not by omission `(heuristic, with the cost measured in §10)`: a crossfade needs the outgoing and the incoming rendering of the whole series in one frame — a second instanced draw of every candle or cell, in a blended pass — and the blend of a green L0 body over a red L2 column produces a colour that is neither, for several frames, on the one layer whose colour means something (`26` §5 rule 3). What makes the step tolerable is the dead band (E11-S12 gate B2: ≤ 6 LOD changes over the 0.1 → 50 px/bar sweep) and the fact that a wheel zoom already changes the picture every frame — the step is one more frame of a moving picture. During a D7 transition that crosses a threshold the step happens mid-flight on the frame the spacing crosses it, exactly as it would under the wheel. The **aggregation chip** (`Zoomed out · 1 column = 12 bars`, E11-D01 D6) and the **reduced-detail corner badge** appear and disappear at 0 ms in both modes (E11-D02 §10 already says "instantly"; D15 makes it the rule for every chip); the ratio text updates in place ≤ 1 per frame with no animation; the chip's entry and exit announcements are E11-D02 §10's (polite once each), unaffected by motion mode. The load-driven step-down (`06` §4.4 rung 2, `lod.stepDownFrames` 3, `lod.recoveryMs` 2 000) is the same one-frame step with a different trigger.

### 6.4 Splitter drag, collapse and restore (D17, D18)

**Drag.** The grip follows the pointer with no easing and the layout re-flows ≤ 1 per frame (coalesced input, `26` §3.11): a splitter that lags lands the boundary somewhere other than where the pointer was released, which the trader then corrects — a drag that needs a second drag `(heuristic)`. The rest → hover → drag → focus states (E11-D02 §8: grip `text.tertiary` → `text.secondary` → `focus.ring`; hairline → `border.strong`) are one-frame colour flips; the keyboard `↑/↓` 2 % and `Shift` 10 % steps are one frame (D8).

**Collapse and restore** (a pane dragged below `max(48 px, 3 × axis label height)`, the pane-label chevron, the stub's restore button) and the splitter's `Home` (equal ratios) / `End` (maximum) **animate the pane heights over `motion.duration.normal`, `motion.easing.standard`** — the only LAYOUT animation in the chassis — because which pane went where is spatial information with two to four candidates and a 200 ms height change keeps the eye on the right one `(heuristic)`. Three rules make it cheap, flicker-free and free of scaling — a **translate + clip**, not a `ky` tween: the collapsing pane's plot is **clipped** to its shrinking rect (its content is not re-laid out per frame); the growing panes are **laid out at their final geometry on the first frame** — plot and ladder, the price range unchanged (under auto-scale it follows the visible window, which a collapse does not change) and the `ky` mapping re-derived once for the final height, under auto-scale and a lock alike — and **revealed by a clip edge** that follows the eased boundary, so no candle is stretched over the 12 frames (the growing pane's vertical mapping changes once, on the first frame, as it does on a resize — §9.4's "no element scales" holds); and **every ladder is computed once per transition, never per frame** (the growing pane's on the first frame, for its final geometry; the same no-flicker rule as §4.3, where the D7 transition's ladders are recomputed on the final frame). The stub's row (label, the word **collapsed**, the restore button) is drawn on the final frame; the announcement ("Volume pane collapsed" / "restored", E11-D02 §8) fires at the start. **Reduce: instant** — the layout changes in one frame, and the information survives in the stub's word, the restore button's name and the announcement. Cost: one LAYOUT pass on the first frame plus a clip rect per pane per frame (§10); shed first under load (D23).

### 6.5 Context-loss recovery (D19)

E11-D04 §12.2's timeline stands verbatim: at 0 s the last frame **stays** and the CMP-028 strip "Graphics context lost — restoring… attempt 1 of 3 · next try in 1 s" appears; 1 s and 3 s update the text; 7 s swaps the strip for the SCR-048 card; any success removes the strip and the chart redraws from `DataModel`. This spec adds the motion rules the strip and the redraw must obey: the strip **appears at 0 ms** (a warning that slides or fades in is a warning read 100–200 ms late); its 1 Hz countdown and the attempt count are **text swaps** (E17-D01 D9: counters are never announced per tick, and here never animated per tick); it carries **no spinner, pulse or progress motion in either mode** — the backoff runs 1, 2, 4, 8 s capped at 30 s, so any loop would run far past `05` §7's 5 s rule, and a static icon plus "next try in N s" says more than a spinner; the "1-frame flash" of `26` §3.4 is read as **the last frame held** behind the strip, never a blank or a cleared canvas (a black frame is exactly the ambiguous state the ticket forbids); and the restore is a **cut**: the restored chart appears at full data-ink in one frame, because a fade-in from dark would look like the stale dim lifting (§6.2) and would teach the trader that "chart restored" and "feed stale" share a look. The strip's text, icon and the "Chart restored" announcement carry the information in both modes; the brief's requirement that a motion-carrying state has an explicit non-animated variant is met here by the state having no motion-safe animation to remove — stated, not implied.

### 6.6 Chips, badges, pills, bands, and the shell recipes the chassis inherits (D15)

Every **state** element of the status row and the chart — the feed chip (`Stale 7s …`, `Reconnecting (3)`, `Offline — retry stopped`, `Reduced rate`), the aggregation chip, the lock badge (`Price scale locked`), the `Paused at hh:mm` pill and the `Live` segment, the replay chip, the countdown, the loading band ("Loading older bars…"), the go-to-date marker and pill, the reduced-detail corner badge, the env badge — **appears, changes and disappears in one frame, in both modes.** E11-D02 §12's default read "tooltip, chip and popover fades (`fast`) → instant" and E11-D03 §12.4 ruled "badge appear / disappear: instant in both modes (a state flip)"; this spec takes the second for every state element `(heuristic)`: a state word at 40 % opacity is a state the trader cannot yet read, the status row flips many times an hour, and the pill grammar (neutral pill = state, warning pill = you are not seeing live, E11-D02 H4) depends on each flip being unambiguous. **Content** surfaces keep the recipes the design system already gives them, confirmed here and owned by E05 / E10 / E11-D03: the CMP-197 tooltip and the status chips' hover / focus tooltips (`fast` fade, `standard`; hover after a 300 ms delay, focus immediately — E11-D03 §12.4), the legend's "+N more" popover (`fast`), the SCR-043 menus (`fast` fade, no slide, no scale), the SCR-044 dialog (`normal` scale + fade, `16` §7) — all instant under reduce. The data-table view switch (`Alt+T`, E11-D04 D1 / D2) has **no transition in either mode** and the host's `suspend()` cancels any coast in flight (D5).

### 6.7 Loading skeleton (D16)

The CMP-029 skeleton (E11-D02 §10 loading: the major / minor grid at the default scale, axis label blocks in `color.surface.hover`, the centred "Loading 5 000 bars…") is the only looping motion in the chassis. `16` §7 names its recipe "`slow` looping shimmer" — a 400 ms token that, read as the loop period, is **2.5 Hz**. That conforms: WCAG 2.3.1 and the `A11Y-G005` gate (`tools/a11y/gates.py`: `FLASH_LUMINANCE_DELTA = 0.1`, `MAX_FLASHES_PER_SECOND = 3`) count a flash only as an opposing relative-luminance change ≥ 0.1 at ≥ 3 per second, and this sweep between `surface.hover` and `surface.raised` (1.17:1) is a ΔL of about 0.01 — so `slow`-as-period passes both. This spec still chooses a longer period, for margin and a calmer loading state `(heuristic)`, and because the recipe's token is a sweep duration whose period `16` §7 never states (A-9). This spec sets the **period to 1,200 ms** (0.83 Hz, PROPOSED), the sweep between the two surface tokens, DOM compositor only, and **stops the loop at 5 s**: past 5 s the skeleton holds the static two-tone and the text carries the progress at ≤ 1 Hz — "Loading 5 000 bars… 7 s", the text-swap pattern rows 15 and 29 use — so the placeholder never reads as frozen while the fetch continues (WCAG 2.2.2's understanding text exempts a preload animation for that reason; `05` §7 item 2 is met either way — nothing auto-moves for more than 5 s without a control, and a loading grid has none). Reduce: the static two-tone from the first frame (`16` §7). The shimmer is paused when the panel is hidden (a background panel animating is work and no one sees it). Amendment A-9 asks `16` §7 to state the period and the cap.

### 6.8 The rest of the chassis: follow-live, the last-price pill, the legend, theme and density, replay, resize

- **Follow-live** (E11-D01 Q1-i): when a bar opens the view shifts by one bar spacing in **one frame**; the kept right offset means nothing else moves. No glide (§4.4).
- **Last-price pill and line** (E11-D02 D7, ORCH-1): the colour follows the **bar's** direction with a ≤ 2/s hold — a one-frame flip, no flash, no fade, the same in both modes; the `16` §7 "value flash" recipe and E47-S07's `flashOnTick` flag are **not applied** to the chart's last-price pill (C-3) — the ▲/▼ glyph carries the direction.
- **Legend eye toggles** (E11-D02 §7.5): a series hidden or shown is an instant redraw in both modes; a fading series would blend with what is under it and the trader toggled it precisely to see what is under it. The eye-off icon, the `text.tertiary` label and the word "hidden" carry the state.
- **Theme, density and text-scale switches** (`setTheme` < 1 ms, `26` §5 rule 1; `setDensity`; A-13 of E11-D02 for text scale): one frame, no cross-fade — a theme cross-fade would need two full renderings and a blended pass, and for 200 ms every colour on the chart would be a colour no token defines. `candleviewer:themechange` arrives on the main thread; the worker applies the new LUT and uniforms at its next frame.
- **Replay** (`26` §10, E11-D02 §10): the frame and the chip are safety elements (D20, §8); the clock is a 1 Hz text swap; the virtual-now line steps per replay tick, not eased (an eased line would mark an instant that has not happened); the future-dim quad is static; at `≥ 20×` speed the view's follow is still one step per bar (frames may show several bars arriving at once — that is the data, not an animation).
- **Resize** (`26` §6, debounced one frame): the layout and the viewport re-derive in one frame; the viewport keeps the right offset and the spacing (bars per pixel), so a resize is never a zoom; no animation in either mode. A coast in flight is cancelled (D5).
- **Sync-group propagation** (E11-S09, `26` §3.5): a member applies the published range in the same frame it arrives (≤ 1 per frame); the remote crosshair likewise (§5.1); a member never integrates its own coast or runs its own transition for a jump another chart made — it receives the leader's per-frame range, so the group moves together and lands together.

## 8. The safety rows (D20, D9)

Three rows of §7 are not design preferences but safety rules, and they hold **in both motion modes, under every degradation rung and in every theme**:

1. **The environment badge never transitions** (row 35; #311 security note; `14` §0.5.1; E11-D02 §10 "never transitional"; E30-D01). DEMO → LIVE and LIVE → DEMO change the word, the weight (`medium` → `bold`), the glyph shape (outline → filled dot), the 2 px border token and the LIVE rule in **one frame**. There is no cross-fade between the two treatments, no intermediate opacity, no slide, no colour tween — a frame in which the badge reads as neither, or as a blend, is a frame in which a trader may size an order against the wrong account. The chart does not announce the switch (the env flow does); the region's accessible name changes in the same frame. The same rule applies to the CMP-074 band the badge repeats — E05's recipe for the band must not fade either (recorded for E05 / E30 in §16).
2. **The replay frame and chip never transition** (row 36; `26` §10). Entering or leaving replay adds or removes the 4 px `color.replay.border` frame and the `REPLAY hh:mm:ss` chip in one frame; the chip's clock is a text swap at 1 Hz; the virtual-now line steps with the replay tick and is never eased between ticks (an eased line marks an instant that has not happened); the future-dim quad is present or absent, never fading. A chart that is half in replay does not exist.
3. **The crosshair position never eases or interpolates** (row 8; #311 AC 3; §5.1). Only its appearance (0 ms), its disappearance (`fast` fade with the position frozen) and the magnet snap (0 ms) carry timings; under reduce even the fade is instant. The keyboard cursor column (row 14) and the remote crosshair of a linked chart follow the same rule.

Two further cuts follow from the same reasoning and are listed with the rejected effects in §10: the **theme switch** is one frame (a cross-fade shows 200 ms of colours no token defines) and the **context-restore redraw** is a cut (a fade-in reads as the stale dim lifting).

## 9. Reduced motion: one source, designed per element (D21, D22)

### 9.1 The single source of truth and what it maps onto

The engine never asks the browser whether the user prefers reduced motion. It consumes **one resolved value** — the `16` §4 runtime contract's motion mode: `data-motion` = `safe | reduce` on the document root and the `candleviewer:themechange` event's `detail.motion` (E05-T02; `packages/ui/src/theme/store.ts` `THEME_CHANGE_EVENT`; `prepaint.ts` writes it before first paint as `override === "on" || (override !== "off" && prefers-reduced-motion)`). That contract already encodes the precedence E11-D01 Q1-c asked this spec to rule on: **the in-app override `system | on | off` wins over `prefers-reduced-motion` in both directions** — `off` gives full motion on a machine whose OS says reduce, `on` gives reduce on one that does not — which is `16` §4's text and the shipped runtime, so it wins over E11-S04's Scope sentence ("any of them being reduce disables momentum; the engine never overrides a user preference in the other direction") and that sentence is amended (A-11). The host forwards the mode to the engine as E11-T04's `theme.reducedMotion` (through `command: setTheme`; and in `init`, because the event does not fire for the initial state — `store.getSnapshot()` does, G-5), so E11-T04's "single engine-wide source of truth" and `16` §4's single store are the same value at two ends of one wire.

The engine already has a motion surface on `main`: E47-S07's `RenderFlags { animate, heatmapFade, inertia, flashOnTick }` on `ChartHandle.setFlags()`. The mapping is fixed here so there is one truth and not two: `theme.reducedMotion = true` ⇒ `{ animate: false, inertia: false, flashOnTick: false, heatmapFade: false }`; `false` ⇒ the flags follow SCR-117's per-flag toggles (which may switch any flag **off** under motion-safe: "disable canvas animation" is a legitimate separate preference) and **nothing switches a flag on under reduce**. `animate` governs the engine's GL rows 5, 10, 18–19, 28; the DOM rows 12, 31, 39–40 are outside `RenderFlags` and follow the same resolved mode through `data-motion` CSS (`16` §4 / §7), so one value drives both; `inertia` governs row 2; `flashOnTick` governs nothing on the chart (row 37, C-3); `heatmapFade` governs E19's decay trail, outside this spec. The flags take effect at the next frame (E47-S07 AC), which is D21's mid-flight rule: a coast stops where it is, a transition jumps to its target, a fade completes.

### 9.2 The keyboard path and the reduce path coincide — deliberately

The #311 a11y note says it and the table shows it: every keyboard **step** is one frame in both modes (rows 4, 14, 27, 44), the keyboard has no momentum, and the only keyboard motion that differs between modes is the **jump** (row 5 — `Home`, `End`, `Ctrl+Home`, cluster `Enter`), which cuts under reduce exactly as the pointer-initiated jump does. So a keyboard-only user under motion-safe sees the same chart as a pointer user under reduce except for the jump cue, and a keyboard-only user under reduce sees exactly the reduce chart. E11-S04's keyboard-help text states that inertia is off under reduce (its AC), in the copy of §16.

## 10. Performance: the register and the shedding order (D23)

## 17. Summary for #311 (≤ 10 lines)

1. The motion of the SCR-030 chassis is fixed element by element in a 45-row table (§7): element, trigger, property, duration token, easing token, reduced-motion variant and whether it carries information; 10 rows animate (five GL rows under the engine's `animate` flag, four DOM rows under `data-motion`, plus the coast under `inertia`), every one has a designed variant, and the four information-carrying ones name their non-animated carrier.
2. Momentum: `26` §3.5's model kept (τ 120 ms PROPOSED, terminate below 0.02 px/ms), integrated against elapsed time with the exact exponential so the rest position is the closed form `x₀ + v₀·τ` — identical at 30 / 60 / 144 fps and with dropped frames (worked: 360.0 px for a 3 px/ms flick at every rate; the per-frame-multiplier mistake lands at 767 / 383 / 160 px); a 50 ms release EWMA, no coast below 0.3 px/ms or after 100 ms of stillness, a 1.5 W cap, a full cancel list, never on wheel, pinch or keys. The coast is time-axis only (the vertical velocity is zeroed at release); under auto-scale the price range refits per frame, under a lock it is held (T11, O-11).
3. Transitions: one cut rule — animate only if ≤ 3 viewport widths, a scale ratio ≤ 4 and — under a locked range only; skipped under auto-scale — overlapping price ranges, else cut; `normal` for ≤ 1 W, `slow` for 1–3 W, `motion.easing.standard`; a 30,000-bar jump is 150 W and cuts; `fit` (ratio 25) cuts.
4. The crosshair never eases or interpolates; it appears at 0 ms, fades out over `fast` with its position frozen, hides during a drag and the coast that continues it (back when the chart rests or at the next `pointermove`), snaps the magnet at 0 ms with a 2 px release hysteresis; the keyboard cursor column never eases (E06-D03 D10's "may" declined).
5. Countdown: 1 Hz text swap, the warn flip is one instant change of word and weight in both modes; stale: chip at 0 ms, the × 0.85 dim ramps `normal` in and out, instant under reduce (3.55:1 floor measured); LOD: one frame, no crossfade; chips and badges are state flips; collapse at `normal` with frozen ladders, instant under reduce; the restoring strip has no motion and the restore is a cut; the shimmer runs at 1,200 ms and stops after 5 s with the text counting on.
6. Safety: the environment badge and the replay frame change in one frame with no cross-fade in either mode; the theme switch and the context restore are cuts for the same reason.
7. One source of truth: the `16` §4 resolved mode (the in-app override wins both ways — E11-D01 Q1-c resolved) via `theme.reducedMotion`, mapped onto E47-S07's `RenderFlags`; the engine never reads the media query.
8. Performance: no row adds a render pass; four rejected effects are named with their cost; cosmetic motion is shed first in a stated order and momentum is never shed; `FrameStats` gains `reducedMotion` / `momentumActive` (additive).
9. Validation: no interactive build exists, so the feel run is written as a protocol (§11: T1–T11, ranges, sheet, pass criteria), every number is PROPOSED, nothing is recorded as validated; 12 contradictions, 7 contract gaps, 11 open questions and 16 amendments carry owners and dates.
