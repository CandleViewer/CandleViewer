## 1. What this document is

The normative rules a builder reads to make the docked workspace usable on a narrow or short window and on a device that draws slowly, and to make a restoring workspace show what is ready. It states each rule once with its reason. It does not cover the non-E15 causes of SCR-153 (exchange feed, recorder, rule engine: E08, E16, E35 own them and share CMP-092's vocabulary), the measurement code (E15-K01, #371), tablet or phone layouts (out of the product), or motion (E15-D14).

## 3. The tabbed collapse (BQ-06; A41)

1. **One group.** Under 1280 px wide every leaf of the stored tree is shown as one tab of a single CMP-251 DockTabGroup filling the dock area, in the tree's reading order (row before column, start before end); the members of an existing tab group stay adjacent in their order. A floating window (SCR-027) is untouched. The stored layout never changes: nothing is saved, the layout is not marked dirty, and widening shows the arrangement exactly as it was.
2. **The active tab** is the panel focused at the moment of collapse; at a launch that starts narrow, the layout's last focused panel, else the first in reading order. Widening restores the arrangement with focus on the same panel; a maximised panel's state returns with it.
3. **Reaching every panel.** The tab row is CMP-251's tablist (arrows, `Home`, `End`, automatic activation); `Ctrl+\` and `F6` still move between the chrome regions; the panel picker is unchanged. A panel never mounted (a launch or a restore that starts narrow) mounts on its first activation (E15-D02 D-14); a hidden tab keeps E15-D03 N-5's 30 s unsubscription of its visual topics.
4. **Overflow.** When the tabs do not fit, labels shorten from "Chart · BTCUSDT 1m" to the kind's glyph plus the symbol (the word kept in the accessible name); if they still do not fit, a "{n} more panels" button at the row's end opens a menu of the rest, each entry with its pending conditions (§5). A horizontally scrolling tab row is not used: a tab out of view carries no count.
5. **What the transform costs.** No WebGL context is created or lost by the collapse or the restore (E15-D03 N-7). A panel already mounted stays mounted behind its tab (CMP-251's inactive tabs stay mounted and hidden, `D13`:423); a panel never mounted mounts on its first activation and creates its context then, never at the collapse.
6. **Focus at the collapse and on widening.** Focus stays on the element that held it while that element is still shown: a resize is not a reason to move it (`05`:96, focus is never silently stolen). If it held a control the collapse removes — a splitter, a header handle or a drop zone, each a stop in E15-D03's `Tab` order (`D03`:786) — it moves to the active tab, which is the panel focused before the collapse (item 2; a splitter counts as the panel it follows). The tab, not the panel's heading, because CMP-251's tablist is the one `Tab` stop on the active tab (`D13`:423) and the panel's region is not a stop of its own: the tab is the nearest place from which every panel is reached. An open menu whose button folds away (the toolbar's buttons at W3) closes, and focus goes to the ⋯ button that now holds it. The move is announced once, politely, by CP-12 in §10's order; no other focus moves. On widening, focus on a tab moves to the same panel's first header control (`D03`:786's order); focus anywhere else stays.

## 5. Hidden tabs: nothing fails in the background (BQ-08; A42)

1. **A tab's name carries its panel's pending conditions** after its region name, in a fixed order — an error, then disconnected or stale, then unread alerts ("Positions, 1 alert"). A position without its native stop (missing, failed to attach) is an error and comes first among them ("Positions, error: 1 position without a stop"), so a hidden Positions tab is never silent about protection. The visible tab shows the registry glyph and the word for an error and a CMP-012 count joined to the name; never colour alone. The overflow button sums its tabs ("3 more panels, 1 with an error"). **Where the conditions come from:** the connection state, the private account state (orders, positions and their protection) and the alert stream, none of which a hidden tab unsubscribes — never the panel's own visual topics, which E15-D03 N-5 unsubscribes after 30 s (`D03`:798), so an unsubscribed tab never reads as stale for that reason. A tab's name changes at most once a second, carrying the latest count (`05` §6.2's latest-value rule); the name is not announced again while the tab has focus.
2. **Account-level events are not panel-bound.** A fill, a rejection, a rule trigger, an automatic flatten and a protection change (a position's native stop missing, failed to attach or set) keep their global surfaces and `05` §6.2's assertive announcements whether or not their panel is visible; while the panel is hidden, its own assertive message (E30-D02 D18's sustained alert for a pending stop, `E30D02`:143) speaks through the shell's assertive region, and the tab carries the error (item 1).
3. **Panel-local states mark the tab.** A restored panel known to fail (a delisted symbol) is marked from the layout document and the instrument metadata before it mounts.
4. **An armed order-capable panel hidden by the collapse** (A42): its tab carries the environment badge (CMP-075) and the arm word; the client asks the server to disarm it on hide and shows the server's answer — the disarm authority is the server's (C-2.21, C-12.4), and the design never arms or disarms by itself; the disarm is announced in A30's form — assertive, once per disarm, with its reason, in the shell's assertive region (LR-07, `E30D05`:295, :328), ahead of CP-12 and never merged into a polite or plural string (§10) —: "One-click trading disarmed: {Kind} hidden in a tab."; trading hotkeys act only on the visible, focused panel and never reach a hidden tab. Under the collapse a panel is hidden by the collapse itself and again whenever the active tab changes away from it; each hide requests the disarm. **Until the answer** the tab keeps the arm word the server last sent, nothing says "disarmed", and the hotkeys still never reach the tab. **If the answer fails** (offline, refused, no answer) the tab keeps the arm word, and a global assertive message says so with a route to the panel (CP-23, account-level as in item 2); the client asks again at the next hide. Re-arming is the user's act on the visible panel, as anywhere else. "Trading hotkeys" here are the order-placing and arming chords; the protective chords are item 5's.
5. **Protective actions at every width** (C-2.6; E30-D05 R-4, `E30D05`:75: a cancel, a close and a stop that is attached or tightened never need the arm). Cancel, close, flatten and setting, tightening or attaching a stop are exits: never gated by the arm, the collapse or the visible-panel rule, and never behind a hidden tab without a route. FLATTEN ALL…, CANCEL ALL… and FREEZE… stay in the Safety cluster in the shell at every width (`D02`:175; never shed, §6.4). A single order's cancel, a position's close and its stop live in their panels (SCR-063, the DOM, the chart, the ticket; §4's rows), one tab switch away, and a position without its stop marks its tab (item 1); E30-D02 D18's Flatten now and Set stop by hand stay "always available" in the ticket. **Account-scoped protective chords work from any tab:** the kill switch's `Ctrl+Shift+K` (PROPOSED in E39-D03 §7, "anywhere": it opens SCR-072 and never engages, `E39D03`:131), and any account-scoped cancel-all or flatten chord the keymap gains.
