The assertive channel: order and no loss (BQ-4, BQ-5; T AC2, T AC4; R-3) > 5.1 The queue#1	l1	Each assertive event enters one queue with its rank (§4): **1** the auto-flatten; **2** the other safety rows (kill switch, native-SL failure, lockout, unwind incomplete); **3** execution (fills, rejects, unknown, trade groups, rule actions); **4** connectivity and environment. The queue is drained once per frame, so events of one frame are ordered by rank, then by arrival. The head is written alone and in full; the next write waits for the head's speech estimate, `max(1.5 s, characters ÷ 15 per second)` (PROPOSED, no owner figure; D07-Q3), and a message being spoken is never cut: "auto-flatten first" holds among the events pending together (D07-C12). T-17's write is its dialog taking focus (§9): the reader's focus speech is the queue's item, and the next write waits for its estimate. The estimate is a guess at the reader's rate: a slower rate, a braille display or a reader that pauses on punctuation can overrun it, and the page cannot observe an overrun ("replaced while speaking"), so the drop counter cannot count it; the log entry is the fallback of record (R-8), and the session protocol listens at 100 % and 60 % of the reader's default rate (hand-off §I; D07-Q3). Assertive writes bypass E17-D02's 5 s region budget, which is ambient-only (D17:249, D07-C14). Each assertive write drops the ambient values waiting for the cadence (PW:116–120): the next composite carries fresh values. Nothing is ever dropped from the queue; the drop counter must stay 0 (§12). The shipped announcer keeps one message and a later write replaces it (ANN:23–24, :38–46): the queue is D07-G1, a G-row to E10-S01 (#304).
